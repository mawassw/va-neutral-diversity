# MWPG 2026 — poster script

Written for the **forward framing** (`CLAUDE.md` Amendment F8), which is what the sheet
says. Two scripts: the **walk-up** (~45 s) and the **tour** (~5 min at the sheet). Then
the questions you will get.

Voiced against `job_packet/walid_authorial_style_guide.md` — "Because" and "Yet" openers,
mechanism-first subjects, colon-delivered definitions, named systems over abstract
categories, short punches after long setups. Register is set for the room: EEB and
genetics, undergrad through faculty. π, N_e, recombination, census size, additive variance
and pedigrees are all fair game. **Nobody in that room needs to know who Santiago and
Caballero are to follow a single claim here.**

**Before you use this.**

1. **Scaffolding, not your voice** (`talk/JOB_TALK_RULES.md` §3). The moves are yours; the
   sentences still are not. Say them out loud once and fix what your mouth refuses.
2. **The claim is forward.** You report how much diversity the reported V_A removes, and
   it is far too little. You are not claiming anything exceeds a limit.

**Four phrasings that lose you the result:**

- *"published estimates exceed the ceiling"* — they don't, at any realistic δ. Zero of 19
  at δ ≤ 0.3. That was the old framing and it is withdrawn.
- *"two to three orders of magnitude"* — impossible in this model. The whole effect caps
  near twentyfold.
- *"either not heritable or not directional"* — withdrawn. A sign flip does not give back
  lost heterozygosity, so reversing selection buys no exemption.
- *"people think large V_A means large adaptive potential"* as a position you refute — the
  careful literature knows better. Informal reading, nobody named, nothing leaning on it.

## 1 · The walk-up (~45 seconds)

> **The hook — while they are still deciding whether to stop.**
> Heritable variance in fitness erodes neutral diversity. Pedigrees measure that variance
> without touching a single base. So there is one estimate of this quantity that comes
> from outside the sequence, and I pushed it through to see what it does.

> **The number.**
> Nineteen wild vertebrate pedigrees, Soay sheep and bighorn and red deer and great tits
> among them. Their reported values remove at most twentyfold of neutral diversity. The
> deficit those species actually carry runs one to four orders deeper.

> **Why the input matters.**
> Because every other estimate of how hard linked selection presses on diversity is
> calibrated on diversity itself — B-maps, DFEs from frequency spectra, sweep fitting.
> Diversity goes in and selection parameters come out, and then you predict diversity.
> Pedigree V_A never touches sequence. That is the whole reason to bother with it.

> **And the reusable half.**
> Run it the other way and diversity sets an upper limit on how much fitness variance a
> population can sustain. Anyone can apply that to their own system. Every value published
> so far stays under it, and I say so on the sheet.

**Then stop, and hand them the choice:**
"Happy to show you where the twentyfold comes from, or the part I think is genuinely
open — which is more useful?"

### 15-second version, for someone on their way past

"Fitness variance and neutral diversity are convertible, and pedigrees give you the
fitness side without any sequence. Do the conversion and the reported values remove at
most twentyfold. The deficit runs one to four orders deeper. So the force is real and
small, and that is a number nobody had."

### The one extra sentence worth memorising

For when someone already has the objection loaded.

"The obvious version of this argument doesn't work, by the way. People say we would notice
if fitness variance were that large, because mean fitness would run away. Yet it wouldn't,
because Fisher's deterioration-of-the-environment term absorbs it: mean fitness sits flat
while the variance stays real and directional. The diversity route survives that escape.
That is why I use it."

## 2 · The tour (~5 minutes)

Left column, then right: **hero → A → B → C → D → the bottom banner.** A and B are one
expression read in two directions. C and D are where the number comes from.

### At the hero card (~30 s)

Point at the rust bar.

> "One sentence, then four checks on it. Take the fitness variance these pedigrees report,
> ask what it does to neutral diversity, and it comes out one to four orders short of what
> is actually missing.
>
> Second line is the flip side. Diversity also sets an upper limit on sustained fitness
> variance, and every value reported so far stays under it. I would rather hand you a limit
> that is true and loose than one that is tight and wrong."

### Panel A — "Diversity erodes without linkage" (~75 s)

Point at the equation, then the figure.

> "Start with the version that assumes almost nothing. Some individuals leave more
> offspring than others, and if any of that difference is heritable, then the lineages that
> persist are a biased sample. Diversity drains faster than the census size would suggest.
>
> Here is the part people don't expect. This needs no linkage at all. Because a neutral
> site's association with the background it happens to sit on halves each generation rather
> than vanishing, the effect survives free recombination and sums over generations. So
> there is a floor on the damage that cares nothing about your genetic map, your
> annotation, or whether anything sits at equilibrium.
>
> Nineteen wild pedigrees, from Bonnet and colleagues in 2022. V_A here is additive
> variance in *relative* fitness, so it is dimensionless: reported values run 0.003 to
> 0.50, median near 0.10.
>
> And the first honest number. Those values buy a threefold reduction in diversity at the
> outside, 1.4-fold at the median. Dashed line is no loss, dotted is tenfold. Nothing
> reaches the dotted line."

**Do not get this backwards.** The predictions sit *above* the dotted line. Anyone reading
the axis will catch you. The point of the panel is that the assumption-free effect is small.

### Panel B — "Diversity sets the upper limit" (~60 s)

> "Before I add anything, invert it. Instead of asking what V_A does to diversity, ask what
> diversity permits for V_A. One minus delta, over four kappa delta.
>
> Delta is on the x axis, and it is worth being exact: the fraction of neutral diversity
> still standing. Observed π over four mu N. Derived, not measured — it needs a mutation
> rate and a census, and those are the two softest numbers in the whole comparison. I would
> rather say that than bury them inside a symbol.
>
> The curve runs the opposite way from most people's first guess. A population that has
> already lost most of its diversity can afford a large V_A, because there is little left
> to protect. One that kept most of its diversity can afford almost none.
>
> Rust is what is actually reported. At a delta of 0.1 the limit sits at 2.25, and the
> largest reported value is 0.50. So the limit is real, it is cheap to compute, and nothing
> anyone has published so far comes up against it."

That last sentence earns you more in this room than a violation would.

### Panel C — "Linkage only tightens it" · MAP-DEPENDENT (~60 s)

> "Now add linkage, because this is where the magnitude comes from. Near a selected site
> the association survives far longer than one generation of recombination allows, so the
> effect grows. Adding recombination maps and functional target sizes at the chromosome
> level only tightens the estimate: a further 1.2 to tenfold on top of Panel A.
>
> Twentyfold, at the outside, for everything together.
>
> I labelled the panel map-dependent rather than supporting, because it carries the
> headline number and it is the part that needs assumptions — maps, annotations, a
> selective background that holds roughly still. Panel A needs none of those. The robust
> claim and the big claim are different claims, and both are on the sheet."

### Panel D — "Far short of the missing diversity" (~45 s)

> "And here is the comparison. Even counting all of the reported V_A as sustained, the most
> generous reading available, the prediction sits above the diversity these populations
> actually keep. The distance to the dotted line is what this force fails to explain.
>
> Then run it down: count half, a quarter, a tenth as the sustained kind. The prediction
> moves the wrong way. The gap widens rather than closing.
>
> Whatever is depleting the diversity in these populations, this is not it."

### The bottom banner — "What these models need V_A to be" (~60 s)

Point at the two boxes.

> "Which brings up the question the whole thing turns on. The calculation I just did and
> Fisher's theorem draw on one quantity: fitness variance that is transmitted and
> sustained.
>
> Left box. Neutral diversity responds only to variance maintained over coalescent time,
> thousands of generations. A pedigree gives you a few decades. Those are not obviously the
> same number, and I would rather put that on the poster than have it raised at me.
>
> Right box. So ask it a different way. Mutation and selection can sustain only so much
> variance, and that route reaches the same question with no diversity data anywhere — once
> the scales are reconciled, which is not trivial, because the variance in those models is
> not defined the way V_A in relative fitness is."

Point at the takeaway column.

> "Three things to take. Any estimate of fitness variance, pedigree or sequence, has to come
> in under that limit; that is the reusable part of this. Reported values remove twentyfold
> at the outside, with nothing on the input side coming from diversity data — the number is
> the finding, not the inequality.
>
> Third one is a discussion point and I will flag it as that. Large measured V_A gets read
> as large adaptive potential. Yet that reading needs the variance to persist, and nothing
> here shows it does."

### Closing line — then stop talking

> "Short version: this is the one estimate of heritable fitness variance that does not come
> from diversity data, and pushed through, it accounts for a small fraction of the diversity
> that is missing. Worth knowing whichever way it had come out."

### 3-minute cut, if there is a queue

Hero → A (no linkage needed; at most threefold) → C (a further 1.2 to tenfold, twentyfold
total) → D (far short) → the "true and loose" sentence from B. Skip the banner.

## 3 · Questions you will get

**"Isn't pedigree V_A biased upward?"** — and this one runs your way.
Probably, yes. Relatives share environments as well as alleles, and animal models count
that resemblance as genetic. Yet look at the direction it pushes: if the true transmissible
variance is smaller than reported, the effect on diversity is weaker still and my
conclusion tightens. I am using the most generous available reading of these estimates and
the force is *still* too small. That is why I lead with it.

**"So you're confirming Buffalo 2021."**
Same direction, different input, and that is the point. Buffalo's route and every route
like it estimates linked selection from diversity data: B-maps, DFEs from frequency
spectra, sweep fitting. A negative result from inside that circle cannot separate "linked
selection is weak" from "our diversity-calibrated estimates of it are too small." Pedigree
V_A never touches sequence, so this asks the same question from outside. It agrees, which
is worth more than a disagreement I had to manufacture.

**"Which N_e do you mean?"** — the sharpest question in the room. Have it cold.
Delta is derived, not measured: observed π over four mu N, with N the breeding adults. And
the N_e that π gives me is an average over roughly the last few times N_e generations, not
a per-generation quantity. That is deliberate, because π registers only fitness variance
that was sustained, and sustained is exactly what the theory takes as input. A contemporary
N_e from linkage disequilibrium would match the pedigree's timescale, but it would test
something close to a tautology and say nothing about diversity.

**"Couldn't you get N from the pedigrees? They record who bred."**
They do, and I use them — for the fraction, not the count. The archive gives breeding
females per cohort directly, and the medians run 15 to 273. Yet that is the monitored study
population, while π reflects something far larger, so using it as the denominator puts delta
in the tens to thousands and every population becomes uninformative. What the pedigrees are
good for is calibrating the breeder fraction empirically, which then scales a species-level
census. They also give me realised variance in lifetime breeding success, which is an
independent check on V_A itself.

**Volunteer the bias direction before anyone finds it.**
Soay sheep on St Kilda, hihi in New Zealand, bighorn in Alberta — small, recently
bottlenecked, closely monitored. So π reflects a larger ancestral gene pool, and immigration
into the study area inflates it further. Both make delta look bigger, which makes the
observed deficit look smaller, which makes my gap look smaller than it is. The main biases
on the observed side run against my own conclusion. There is also a screening rule: if delta
comes out at or above one, the neutral counterfactual failed before selection entered and
that population drops out.

**"Connallon and Czuppon showed fluctuating and balancing selection maintain a lot of V_A."**
They did, and it is a good result. Yet it answers a different question: that work is about
how high V_A gets *maintained*, and I am not trying to explain the maintenance. I take the
measured value as input and ask what it implies. So it is not a competing resolution of my
premise. Worth adding that a sign flip gives no diversity back either, so reversing
selection exempts nobody from the cost.

**"What about the mutation–selection–drift route?"**
Right next question, and it is the right half of the banner. It asks how much variance
mutation and selection can actually sustain, with no diversity data anywhere, and it has
power where the diversity route has none. Two honest caveats. The variance in those models
is not on the same scale as V_A in relative fitness, and that has to be reconciled
explicitly. And Charlesworth put the qualitative conclusion in print this year, so my
contribution there is the number, not the direction.

**"Is this Lewontin's paradox?"**
It is where the comparison happens, not what the project is about. Take out Lewontin, take
out Buffalo, take out Charlesworth, and a number survives all three: how much diversity
heritable fitness variance can remove, estimated without using diversity to get there.

**"What's kappa?"**
A mating-system factor between one and two, quadratic in the extra-pair paternity rate. It
matters by a factor of two for the monogamous passerines that dominate this dataset, but it
is a modelling detail. Every number I have quoted is at kappa equals one.

**"What's next?"**
Real diversity for these species from Lewin and Eyre-Walker, with a census from Buffalo plus
an allometry for the ones he doesn't cover. That turns Panel B's curve into a per-population
number and pins the deficit down properly. Then reconciling the scale between V_A in
relative fitness and the variance in mutation–selection–drift models, so the second route
can actually be run.
