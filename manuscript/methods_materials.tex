\section{Materials and Methods}
All inputs pass through one reproducible data layer (\texttt{scripts/R/00\_prepare\_data.R}), which joins the sources below through a single population crosswalk and writes the tables every analysis reads.

\subsection{Additive variance in fitness}
Posterior distributions of $V_A$ come from Bonnet et al. (2022): 19 longitudinally monitored wild vertebrate populations, 11 socially monogamous (10 bird populations and the meerkat) and 8 without a pair bond (polygynous or polygynandrous mammals). Bonnet et al. fitted a zero-inflated over-dispersed Poisson animal model to lifetime breeding success, with both sexes in each data set and sex included in the model, and back-transformed the link-scale genetic covariance matrix to the observed scale as $V_A(w) = \mathbf{d}^\top\mathbf{G}\mathbf{d}$. The estimates are therefore already additive variance in relative fitness ($\bar{w} = 1$), the quantity of \autoref{sec:quantity}, and no standardization was applied. For each population we use the 4,000 posterior draws, summarized by the posterior mode and the 95\% highest posterior density interval. The modes run from 0.003 to 0.50 (median 0.10).

\subsection{Mating system}
Each population was classed as socially monogamous or without a pair bond. For socially monogamous populations, the extra-pair paternity rate $\alpha$ was taken from published molecular parentage studies of the same or a conspecific population [author: list sources per population, and state whether $\alpha$ is the proportion of offspring, as Appendix B assumes, or of broods]. The 11 rates run from 0.047 (great tits) to 0.76 (superb fairy-wren). The mating-system scalar is $\kappa = 1$ without a pair bond and $\kappa_B(\alpha)$ (Appendix B, Model B) under social monogamy, giving $\kappa = 1.22$--$1.93$. Model A is the sensitivity bound. All primary results set $G = 1$ and $r_{mf} = 0$. The $G = 1 - V_A$ correction (Appendix A) is reported as a sensitivity analysis, and $r_{mf}$ is set to zero for want of estimates of assortative pairing on fitness.

\subsection{Genome assemblies, functional target size and mutation rates}
Chromosome-level assemblies and annotations were retrieved from NCBI for 18 of the 19 populations; the only superb fairy-wren assembly is unannotated. For each assembled autosome we recorded physical length, the number of annotated protein-coding genes, and coding length $L_{\text{coding},i}$. Coding length was computed by collapsing all CDS features into non-redundant intervals, so that isoforms and overlapping reading frames are not counted twice. Sex chromosomes, mitochondrial sequence and unplaced scaffolds were excluded. Two species lack a chromosome-level annotated assembly: the spotted hyena and the hihi. For these the genome-wide CDS total was scaled by the autosomal CDS share of the nearest chromosome-level relative: 0.965 from the meerkat for the hyena, and 0.948, the mean of four passerines, for the hihi.

Per-site mutation rates were carried under two estimates, not averaged. The first, $\mu_\text{phylo}$, is the phylogenetic mixed-model prediction of Wang and Obbard (2023). The second, $\mu_\text{direct}$, is a direct pedigree or trio estimate for the species or its closest relative, compiled by Lewin and Eyre-Walker (2026). The phylogenetic model is fitted to the direct estimates, so the two are not independent, and averaging their credible bounds would not produce a credible interval. Results use $\mu_\text{direct}$ and are repeated under $\mu_\text{phylo}$, which runs 7--60\% higher. The diploid deleterious mutation rate is $U_d = 4\mu L_\text{coding}$, summed over assembled autosomes (\autoref{msdb}), and runs 0.34--1.26 under $\mu_\text{direct}$.

\subsection{Mutation--selection--drift comparison}
For every posterior draw of $V_A$ we computed $s^* = V_A/U_d$ (Equation~\eqref{eq:sstar}) under both mutation rates, and report its posterior distribution and the posterior probabilities $P(s^* > 0.01)$ and $P(s^* > 0.001)$. These are compared with the mean heterozygous effect of vertebrate DFEs, $10^{-3}$--$10^{-2}$ (Eyre-Walker and Keightley 2007; Huber et al. 2017). We confirmed the strong-selection limit by computing $2N_es^*$, with $N_e = \pi/(4\mu)$, wherever $\pi$ is available. Read forward, $V_A = U_d\,s$ at $s = 10^{-3}$, $5 \times 10^{-3}$ and $10^{-2}$ gives the variance mutation--selection balance can maintain (Figure 1).

\subsection{Linked interference}
Linkage maps were joined to assemblies chromosome by chromosome, by curated linkage-group correspondence. For the song sparrow, whose assembly numbers chromosomes by size, the join goes through gene-symbol orthology to the great tit assembly. Map lengths $M_i$ are in Morgans [author: confirm whether sex-averaged maps were used throughout]. Nine populations have at least one mapped macro-autosome; the others contribute to the unlinked analyses only. Chromosomes shorter than 20 Mb were excluded from the linked term.

Additive variance was partitioned among autosomes in proportion to coding length, $v_i = V_A f_i$ with $f_i = L_{\text{coding},i}/\sum_i L_{\text{coding},i}$; the partition by annotated gene count is reported as a sensitivity analysis [author: confirm which partition is primary]. The variance on the focal haplotype is $v_h = v_i/2$. $\overline{Q^2}_i$ was evaluated from Equation~\eqref{eq:haldane_avg} under the Haldane map, by quadrature, which agrees with the closed form of Appendix C. The persistence $Z = 1 - U_d\,\mathbb{E}[s^2]/V_A$ was evaluated with uniform heterozygous effects $s = 0.005$, $0.01$ and $0.02$, and capped at 0.995. The chromosome-level reduction follows Equation~\eqref{total_red}, with the same $Z$ in the linked and unlinked terms. The genome-wide value weights chromosomes by physical length.

\subsection{Nucleotide diversity and census size}
Neutral autosomal nucleotide diversity $\pi$ is available from Lewin and Eyre-Walker (2026) for 7 of the 19 populations, 5 of them for the study species itself. For four further populations we use published whole-genome estimates from another population of the same species. These are the two great tit populations (Barton and Zeng 2019), bighorn sheep (Wang et al. 2023) and song sparrow (Carbeck et al. 2023). The bighorn and song sparrow values come from MAF-filtered SNP sets and are likely biased low. Single-genome heterozygosity is recorded but not used as $\pi$. Census sizes $N_c$ are those published by Buffalo (2021), for the study species only (10 of 19). They are density $\times$ range estimates. His bird densities rest on body mass predicted from body length and are likely about 1.2 orders of magnitude too low, and his \emph{Ovis aries} value is domestic sheep, not the St Kilda Soay population. No population has a breeding-adult count. Using $N_c$ in place of $N$ lowers $\delta$ by the breeder fraction, a factor of 2--10, which widens the apparent gap. We record the instrument for each population.

Nine populations (six species) have both $\pi$ and $N_c$. For these we compare predicted and observed diversity in $\pi$ units, $\pi_\text{pred} = 4\mu N_c/(1 + 4\kappa V_A)$ against $\pi_\text{obs}$, and report $\delta = \pi/(4\mu N_c)$ only as the axis of the $V_{A,\max}$ curve. Populations with $\delta \ge 1$ would make $V_{A,\max} \le 0$, a failed model check rather than a result, and are reported as uninformative. None of the nine falls in that class.

\subsection{Rescaling the long-run variance}
To ask whether any long-run variance satisfies both comparisons, we write $\bar{V}_A = c\,\hat{V}_A$ (\autoref{sec:quantity}). For each of the nine populations we evaluate two ratios as functions of $c$. The first is $\bar{V}_A/(U_d s)$ at $s = 10^{-2}$, the value most favourable to mutation--selection balance. The second is $(N_e/N)_\text{pred}/\delta$, with $(N_e/N)_\text{pred} = 1/(1 + 4\kappa\bar{V}_A)$: the diversity the model predicts is retained, over the diversity observed to be retained. A comparison is satisfied where its ratio is at most 1. Populations of a species share $\pi$ and $N_c$, so log ratios are averaged within species first. We then report the mean across species with a $t$-based 95\% interval, together with the diversity ratio under $G = 1 - \bar{V}_A$ inside the model's domain ($\bar{V}_A < 1$; Figure 2).

\subsection{Independence of the inputs from diversity data}
None of the model's inputs is estimated from the neutral diversity it is compared against. $V_A$ comes from pedigrees and breeding records (Bonnet et al. 2022). $\alpha$ comes from parentage analyses, $M_i$ from linkage maps, and $f_i$ and $L_\text{coding}$ from genome annotations. $\mu$ comes from direct pedigree or trio sequencing, or from a phylogenetic model fitted to such estimates, and $N_c$ from density and range. The diversity side of the comparison is therefore a prediction tested against $\pi_\text{obs}$, not a fit to it. The one shared source is Lewin and Eyre-Walker (2026), which compiles both $\mu_\text{direct}$ and $\pi$; the two are measured independently.
