\subsection{The genome-wide reduction at the measured variance}\label{sec:res_unlinked}
% Numbers from scripts/R/tab_reduction.R (data/processed/, 2026-10-19). Rerun it after any data change.
Table~\ref{tab:reduction} gives the unlinked reduction in effective size at the posterior mode of each estimate, under the one model of fitness of \autoref{sec:onemodel}. At first order, $N_e/N$ runs from 0.22 (blue tits at Muro) to 0.99, with a median of 0.60: a reduction of at most about fivefold. The multiplicative model agrees with the first-order form wherever $\hat{V}_A$ is small, and departs from it appreciably only in the five populations with $\hat{V}_A$ above about 0.25, the blue tits at Pirio and Muro, spotted hyenas, red deer and bighorn sheep. Its median is 0.51, and its smallest value, 0.021 for the Muro blue tits, is close to the upper bound on the reduction, 0.019.

Nine populations, in six species, have both a measure of neutral diversity and a census size. Their observed diversity ratios $\delta$ run from 1/230 (bighorn sheep) to 1/38,000 (song sparrows). Even under the multiplicative upper bound, the smallest variance that could account for these deficits (Equation~\eqref{eq:vamax_mult}) is 1.1 to 1.7. That exceeds the upper limit of the 95\% HPD interval of every one of the nine estimates, the largest of which is 0.70. At the posterior modes the bound falls short of the observed deficit by factors of 80 (bighorn sheep) to 31,000 (great tits at Hoge Veluwe).

\subsection{Intragenomic Variation in Reduction}
While the expected species-wide diversity, $\mathbb{E}[N_e/N]$, provides a global summary of evolutionary constraint, the continuous spatial framework predicts highly heterogeneous topographies of diversity within the genome. By evaluating the Main Equation across independent chromosomes, we can analytically partition the sources of intragenomic variance.

For a focal chromosome $i$, the genome-wide unlinked baseline generated by independent assortment is captured by the unlinked exponent of Equation~\eqref{total_red}, $Q^2(\kappa V_A - \kappa_\text{own}\,v_i/2)$. Because the variance segregating on a single haplotype represents a minor fraction of the genome-wide total or $v_i/2 \ll V_A$, this unlinked constraint is effectively uniform across the genome. Consequently, any predicted variance in standing neutral diversity between chromosomes is driven strictly by the localized, constraint of physical linkage.

Because the localized selective variance is proportional to the functional target size of the chromosome, i.e. $v_i = V_A f_i$, the survival of neutral lineages is governed by the structural ratio of functional density to recombinational map length. Chromosomes with high coding densities and restricted recombination suffer severe exponential decay in local $N_e$, driving rapid lineage sorting. Conversely, chromosomes with expansive map lengths and sparse functional targets effectively isolate neutral alleles from selective backgrounds. These structural dynamics predict the existence of macro-genomic regions that maintain robust neutral diversity even when the global, genome-wide constraint is strong.
