\section*{Abstract}
Pedigree-based estimates show that wild populations carry substantial additive genetic variance in lifetime fitness. Classical population genetics theory predicts two equilibrium consequences of such variance: it should reduce neutral diversity, and it should be maintained by recurrent deleterious mutation at mutation--selection balance. We test estimates of the additive genetic variance in lifetime fitness from 19 pedigreed populations against both equilibrium models where the data allow (18 in the maintenance test and 9 in the diversity test), under the same assumption of the estimates that genetic effects on fitness are multiplicative. We extend the predicted reduction in effective population size to social monogamy with extra-pair paternity and to linked sites along empirical linkage maps, and compute the variance that measured mutation rates and empirical distributions of fitness effects can maintain in these populations. The measured variance is too small to explain the missing neutral diversity and too large to be maintained by mutation--selection balance. It roughly halves the effective size of the median population, whereas the observed neutral diversity deficits of 230- to 38,000-fold would require a long-term variance above one, larger than any estimate. Maintaining the measured variance would require the average new mutation to lower the fitness of a heterozygous carrier by about 16\%, a median 21 times the mean effects estimated in related species. No single rescaling of the estimates passes both tests as the long-term variance, because the rescalings the two tests require differ about 390-fold on average and over 100-fold in each of the nine populations with diversity data. The conflict between the two equilibria does not depend on the estimates themselves, because in those populations a variance maintained by mutation would remove at most 5\% of neutral diversity at unlinked sites. If the measured variance persists, most of it is therefore maintained by processes other than recurrent deleterious mutation, and most of the missing diversity has causes other than heritable variance in fitness.
