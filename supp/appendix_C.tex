\section{The linked factor along a Haldane map}\label{app:chromosapp}

\subsection{The kernel and chromosome average}\label{app:C_kernel}
Consider a single mutational effect class with retention factor $Z$ (Equation~\eqref{eq:Z}); under a distribution of fitness effects, each expression below is averaged over classes using the variance weights $\nu_k$ of \autoref{sec:linkage}. Take a chromosome of map length $M$ Morgans (dropping the chromosome index $i$ for clarity) on which two sites separated by $\ell$ Morgans recombine with probability $r(\ell)$. Unless stated otherwise (Section~\ref{app:C_spatial}), both selected variance and neutral sites are distributed uniformly in map distance. Let $Q^2 = [2/(2-Z)]^2$ denote the unlinked transmission factor squared (Equation~\eqref{eq:Q}), $\overline{Q^2}(y)$ the local linked value at a neutral site at position $y$ averaged over selected loci, and $\overline{Q^2}_\text{H}$ and $\overline{Q^2}_\text{L}$ the chromosome-wide averages under the Haldane and linear maps ($\overline{Q^2}_\text{H}$ is the $\overline{Q^2}_i$ of the main text).

An association between a neutral site and a selected locus $\ell$ Morgans away survives one generation with probability $(1 - r(\ell))Z$, as recombination breaks it with probability $r(\ell)$ and selection multiplies the remaining association by $Z$. Summing over generations gives the cumulative advantage per unit effect~\cite{santiago2016,buffaloKern2024},
\begin{equation}
    \sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\left[(1 - r(\ell))\,Z\right]^t = \frac{1}{1-(1-r(\ell))Z}.
\end{equation}
Because this cumulative factor enters the variance of long-term contributions squared (as $Q^2$ does in Equation~\eqref{eq:A_first}), a selected locus at distance $\ell$ contributes via the kernel $\mathcal{K}(\ell) = [1 - (1 - r(\ell))Z]^{-2}$. Defining the arm integral $\mathcal{I}(\ell) = \int_0^\ell \mathcal{K}(\ell')\,d\ell'$, the local profile~\cite{santiago2016,buffaloKern2024} and chromosome-wide average are
\begin{gather}
    \overline{Q^2}(y) = \frac{1}{M}\int_0^M \mathcal{K}(\vert x-y\vert)\,dx = \frac{1}{M}\left[\mathcal{I}(y) + \mathcal{I}(M-y)\right], \label{eq:C_profile} \\
    \frac{1}{M}\int_0^M \overline{Q^2}(y)\,dy = \frac{2}{M^2}\int_0^M (M-\ell)\,\mathcal{K}(\ell)\,d\ell = \frac{2}{M^2}\int_0^M \mathcal{I}(\ell)\,d\ell, \label{eq:C_haldane_avg}
\end{gather}
where the factor $(M - \ell)$ weights each distance $\ell$ by the density of site pairs separated by $\ell$. Under Haldane's mapping function, $r_\text{H}(\ell) = \tfrac12(1 - e^{-2\ell})$, Equation~\eqref{eq:C_haldane_avg} yields Equation~\eqref{eq:haldane_avg} of the main text.

\subsection{Closed form under the Haldane map}\label{app:C_closed}
Under Haldane's map, $(1 - r_\text{H}(\ell))Z = \tfrac{Z}{2}(1 + e^{-2\ell})$, so
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{K}_\text{H}(\ell) = \left(\Gamma - \eta\, e^{-2\ell}\right)^{-2}, \qquad \Gamma = 1 - \tfrac{Z}{2}, \quad \eta = \tfrac{Z}{2}.
\end{equation}
Substituting $\tau = e^{-2\ell}$ and decomposing into partial fractions yields the arm integral in elementary functions:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{I}_\text{H}(\ell) = \frac{1}{2}\left\{\frac{2\ell + \ln\!\left[(\Gamma - \eta e^{-2\ell})/(1-Z)\right]}{\Gamma^2} + \frac{1}{\Gamma}\left[\frac{1}{1-Z} - \frac{1}{\Gamma - \eta e^{-2\ell}}\right]\right\}.
\label{eq:C_arm}
\end{equation}
Integrating once more over $[0, M]$, using $\int_0^M \ln(\Gamma - \eta e^{-2\ell})\,d\ell = M\ln \Gamma + \tfrac{1}{2}\left[\mathrm{Li}_2(\epsilon e^{-2M}) - \mathrm{Li}_2(\epsilon)\right]$ with $\epsilon = \eta/\Gamma$ and $\mathrm{Li}_2$ the dilogarithm, gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
    \overline{Q^2}_\text{H} = \frac{1}{M^2}\Bigg\{ &\frac{M^2 - M\ln(1-Z) + M\ln \Gamma + \tfrac{1}{2}\left[\mathrm{Li}_2(\epsilon e^{-2M}) - \mathrm{Li}_2(\epsilon)\right]}{\Gamma^2} \\
    &+ \frac{1}{\Gamma}\left[\frac{M}{1-Z} - \frac{2M + \ln\!\left[(\Gamma-\eta e^{-2M})/(1-Z)\right]}{2\Gamma}\right]\Bigg\}.
\end{split}
\label{eq:C_closed_haldane}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:C_closed_haldane} is the exact form evaluated in the main analysis. Against direct numerical quadrature of Equation~\eqref{eq:C_haldane_avg} in R, Equation~\eqref{eq:C_closed_haldane} agrees to better than $10^{-11}$ relative error across $Z \in [10^{-9}, 1 - 10^{-9}]$ and $M \in [0.005, 60]$ Morgans, spanning all selection classes and map lengths analyzed.

\subsection{Short- and long-map limits}\label{app:C_limits}
As $M \to 0$, $\overline{Q^2}_\text{H} \to (1-Z)^{-2}$, the non-recombining limit of background selection. Approaching this limit requires recombination across the entire chromosome to be slower than purging ($M \ll 1 - Z$), not merely a short physical map: at $Z = 0.99$ and $M = 0.5$, $\overline{Q^2}_\text{H} = 387$ compared with $(1-Z)^{-2} = 10^4$. Conversely, as $\ell \to \infty$, $\mathcal{K}_\text{H}(\ell) \to \Gamma^{-2} = Q^2$, recovering the unlinked factor. Convergence to this free-recombination limit is slow:
\begin{equation}
    \overline{Q^2}_\text{H} = Q^2 + \frac{2}{M}\int_0^\infty \left[\mathcal{K}_\text{H}(\ell) - Q^2\right]d\ell + O(M^{-2}).
\end{equation}
From Equation~\eqref{eq:C_arm}, the leading integral evaluates to $\frac{1}{2\Gamma}\left[\frac{1}{1-Z} - \frac{1}{\Gamma} + \frac{\ln(\Gamma/(1-Z))}{\Gamma}\right] \sim (1-Z)^{-1}$ as $Z \to 1$ ($10.3$ at $Z = 0.9$ and $105$ at $Z = 0.99$). Free recombination therefore requires $M \gg 1/(1-Z)$; even at $M = 10$ and $Z = 0.99$, $\overline{Q^2}_\text{H} = 24.8$ against $Q^2 = 3.9$.

Empirical chromosomes occupy the intermediate regime $\overline{Q^2}_\text{H} \approx 2/[M(1-Z)]$~\cite{santiago1998}. Even avian microchromosomes rarely approach the non-recombining limit: an obligate crossover per bivalent implies $M \gtrsim 0.5$ Morgans, and the shortest empirical maps analyzed here ($0.04$--$0.2$ Morgans in great tits and song sparrows) still greatly exceed $1 - Z$ for all but the most strongly purged mutational classes (Table~\ref{tab:linked_limits}).

\subsection{The linear map as a lower bound}\label{app:C_linear}
Under a linear map, $r_\text{L}(\ell) = \ell$, the kernel is $\mathcal{K}_\text{L}(\ell) = (1 - Z + \ell Z)^{-2}$ and the arm integral is $\mathcal{I}_\text{L}(\ell) = \ell/[(1-Z)(1-Z+\ell Z)]$, whose mid-chromosome value matches Supplementary Equation~33 of Buffalo and Kern~\cite{buffaloKern2024}. Equation~\eqref{eq:C_haldane_avg} gives
\begin{equation}
    \overline{Q^2}_\text{L} = \frac{2}{M^2(1-Z)}\int_0^M \frac{y}{1-Z+yZ}\,dy = \frac{2}{MZ(1-Z)} - \frac{2}{M^2Z^2}\ln\!\left(1+\frac{MZ}{1-Z}\right).
\label{eq:closed_form}
\end{equation}
As $M \to 0$, $\overline{Q^2}_\text{L} = (1-Z)^{-2} - \tfrac{2}{3}MZ(1-Z)^{-3} + O(M^2)$, matching $\overline{Q^2}_\text{H}$ to first order because $r_\text{H}(\ell) = \ell + O(\ell^2)$. As $M \to \infty$, however, $\overline{Q^2}_\text{L} \to 0$ rather than $Q^2$, because $r_\text{L}(\ell)$ exceeds $1/2$ for $\ell > 1/2$.

Because $e^{-2\ell} > 1 - 2\ell$ implies $r_\text{H}(\ell) < \ell$ for all $\ell > 0$, it follows that $0 < 1 - Z + r_\text{H}(\ell)Z < 1 - Z + \ell Z$ everywhere for $Z \in (0, 1)$, so $\mathcal{K}_\text{L}(\ell) < \mathcal{K}_\text{H}(\ell)$ at every distance and
\begin{equation}
    \overline{Q^2}_\text{L} < \overline{Q^2}_\text{H} \qquad \text{for all } M > 0.
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:closed_form} is thus a strict lower bound on $\overline{Q^2}_i$ and on the linked reduction in $N_e$. The ratio $\overline{Q^2}_\text{L}/\overline{Q^2}_\text{H}$ declines with both $M$ and $1 - Z$, taking values $0.98$, $0.95$, and $0.91$ at $M = 0.1$, $1$, and $3$ Morgans for $Z = 0.99$, and $0.99$, $0.85$, and $0.67$ for $Z = 0.9$. By contrast, the mid-segment linear approximation used by Buffalo and Kern~\cite{buffaloKern2024}, $\overline{Q^2}_\text{L}(M/2) = 1/[(1-Z)(1-Z+ZM/2)]$, exceeds $\overline{Q^2}_\text{H}$ by up to $12\%$ on short maps ($M$ a few multiples of $1 - Z$) and falls below it on longer maps under stronger purging ($0.81$ at $M = 10$, $Z = 0.99$; $0.41$ at $M = 10$, $Z = 0.9$; Figure~\ref{fig:S_maps}). Both linear approximations remain accurate across the short, densely mapped windows for which they were designed, while the main text uses the exact Haldane form (Equation~\eqref{eq:C_closed_haldane}).

\subsection{Upper bound on the linked factor (\texorpdfstring{$\Omega_i \le 1$}{Omega <= 1})}\label{app:omega}
Because $\mathcal{K}_\text{H}(\ell)$ decreases monotonically from $(1-Z)^{-2}$ to $Q^2$, every local profile satisfies $\overline{Q^2}(y) \ge Q^2$, and therefore $\overline{Q^2}_i \ge Q^2$ regardless of how selected variance is distributed along the chromosome (Section~\ref{app:C_spatial}). We define three compound quantities
\begin{equation}
    \Lambda_\text{linked} = \kappa_\text{cis}\overline{Q^2}_iv_h, \qquad \Lambda_\text{own} = \kappa_\text{own}Q^2v_h, \qquad \Lambda_\text{unlinked} = Q^2\kappa V_A.
\end{equation}
In the primary model $\kappa_\text{cis} = \kappa_\text{own}$ (Appendix~\ref{app:B_split}), so $\Lambda_\text{linked} \ge \Lambda_\text{own}$; furthermore, $\Lambda_\text{unlinked} \ge \Lambda_\text{own}$ since $v_h \le V_A$ and $\kappa \ge \kappa_\text{own}$. Under the multiplicative form of Equation~\eqref{total_red}, $\Omega_i = e^{-(\Lambda_\text{linked} - \Lambda_\text{own})} \le 1$ follows immediately, with equality on a freely recombining chromosome ($\overline{Q^2}_i \to Q^2$).

The bound also holds when the unlinked factor uses the small-variance reciprocal form $1/(1 + \Lambda_\text{unlinked} - \Lambda_\text{own})$: since $e^{-\Lambda_\text{linked}} \le e^{-\Lambda_\text{own}} \le 1/(1+\Lambda_\text{own})$,
\begin{equation}
    \Omega_i = e^{-\Lambda_\text{linked}}\,\frac{1+\Lambda_\text{unlinked}}{1+\Lambda_\text{unlinked}-\Lambda_\text{own}} \le \frac{1+\Lambda_\text{unlinked}}{(1+\Lambda_\text{own})(1+\Lambda_\text{unlinked}-\Lambda_\text{own})} \le 1,
\end{equation}
where the final inequality holds because $(1+\Lambda_\text{own})(1+\Lambda_\text{unlinked}-\Lambda_\text{own}) - (1+\Lambda_\text{unlinked}) = \Lambda_\text{own}(\Lambda_\text{unlinked}-\Lambda_\text{own}) \ge 0$. Under free recombination, this mixed form gives $\Omega_i = 1 - \Lambda_\text{own}\Lambda_\text{unlinked}/(1 + \Lambda_\text{unlinked}) + O(\Lambda_\text{own}^2)$, an $O(v_h V_A)$ discrepancy arising solely from combining exponential and reciprocal approximations for the same variance. Under a distribution of fitness effects, the bound applies class by class.

\subsection{Non-uniform placement of selected variance}\label{app:C_spatial}
Because the chromosomal architecture of fitness variance in wild populations is unknown~\cite{barton2002}, uniform placement is a baseline assumption. Let $g(x) \ge 0$ ($\int_0^M g(x)\,dx = 1$) denote the density of selected variance in map distance. With neutral sites uniform in map distance, symmetry of $\mathcal{K}_\text{H}(\vert x-y\vert)$ yields
\begin{equation}
    \overline{Q^2}_\text{H}[g] = \frac{1}{M}\int_0^M\!\!\int_0^M g(x)\, \mathcal{K}_\text{H}(\vert x-y\vert)\,dx\,dy = \int_0^M g(x)\,\overline{Q^2}(x)\,dx, \label{general_g}
\end{equation}
recovering $\overline{Q^2}_\text{H}$ when $g = 1/M$ and the average $\frac{1}{J}\sum_{j=1}^J \overline{Q^2}(x_j)$ for $J$ equal-effect loci at $x_1, \dots, x_J$. Because $\overline{Q^2}(x) \ge Q^2$ everywhere, $\Omega_i \le 1$ holds for every $g$. Moreover, $\overline{Q^2}''(x) = [\mathcal{K}_\text{H}'(x) + \mathcal{K}_\text{H}'(M-x)]/M < 0$, so the profile is strictly concave, peaking at the midpoint $M/2$ (linked to both arms) and attaining its minimum at the telomeres ($0$ and $M$).

Because linked diversity depends on the expectation of the convex function $\exp(-\kappa_\text{cis} v_h \overline{Q^2})$, Jensen's inequality implies that for a single locus placed uniformly at random on $[0, M]$,
\begin{equation}
    \mathbb{E}_{x_0}\!\left[\exp\!\left(-\kappa_\text{cis} v_h \overline{Q^2}(x_0)\right)\right] > \exp\!\left(-\kappa_\text{cis} v_h \overline{Q^2}_\text{H}\right).
\end{equation}
For $J$ independently placed equal-effect loci, a second-order expansion gives
\begin{equation}
    \mathbb{E}\!\left[\exp\!\left(-\kappa_\text{cis} v_h \overline{Q^2}_\text{H}[g]\right)\right] - \exp\!\left(-\kappa_\text{cis} v_h \overline{Q^2}_\text{H}\right) \approx \frac{(\kappa_\text{cis} v_h)^2}{2J}\exp\!\left(-\kappa_\text{cis} v_h \overline{Q^2}_\text{H}\right)\mathrm{Var}_x\!\left[\overline{Q^2}(x)\right].
\end{equation}
Under random placement independent of recombination rate, uniform density therefore maximizes the expected linked reduction in $N_e$ and is conservative with respect to understating linkage. This ordering can reverse, however, when selected alleles are concentrated in regions of suppressed recombination such as pericentromeres or chromosomal inversions. Even without physical clustering, low recombination compresses large stretches of selected and neutral sequence into a short map interval, locking them together and elevating local linkage above the uniform-map baseline; evolutionary processes that actively concentrate selected alleles within low-recombination regions or inversions~\cite{kirkpatrick2006,yeaman2013} compound this effect further.
