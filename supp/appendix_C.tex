\section{Effective size with separate sexes, pair bonds and extra-pair paternity}\label{app:matingapp}
This appendix derives the mating-system scalar $\kappa$ of Equation~\eqref{unlinked_mating} \claude{(Model B, Equation}~\eqref{eq:kappaB}\claude{)}, its split into the components used in Equation~\eqref{total_red}, and the multiplicative-model bracket of Equation~\eqref{eq:C_mult_pair}. It applies the expected long-term contributions of Woolliams, Bijma, and Villanueva~\cite{woolliams1999} and the two-sex combination of Santiago and Caballero~\cite{santiago1995}. 

Consider a diploid population with discrete generations, $N$ breeding adults, and an equal sex ratio. Each individual possesses an additive breeding value $a$ for relative lifetime breeding success, with variance $\mathrm{Var}(a) = V_A$ assumed equal in both sexes. For small advantages, $a = z - \bar{z}$ (Appendix~\ref{app:unlinkedapp}). Bonnet et al.~\cite{bonnet2022} estimate this single variance from an animal model incorporating both sexes with sex fitted as a fixed effect.

Under random mating, a parent's relative family size is $1 + a$. Under social monogamy, the nest serves as the unit of productivity. A female paired with social partner $m'$ has relative family size $1 + u_f = 1 + a_f + a_{m'}$. Extra-pair paternity at rate $\alpha$ moves paternity out of the nest without altering its overall productivity, so a male paired with social partner $f'$ achieves relative success
\begin{equation}
    u_m = (1-\alpha)(a_m + a_{f'}) + \alpha\gamma\, a_m,
\end{equation}
where $\gamma$ indexes the dependence of extra-pair siring success on the male's own breeding value. Model A sets $\gamma = 0$ (extra-pair siring as a random lottery), whereas Model B sets $\gamma = 1$ (extra-pair gains exactly offset within-pair losses). Model B is the unique parameterization in which a male's breeding value contributes to his total fitness with the same average weight as a female's across all $\alpha$, satisfying the single-variance assumption of the animal model. Model B is therefore adopted for all primary results. 

A carrier's own breeding value therefore contributes to its advantage with weight $1$ in females and $\beta = 1 - \alpha + \alpha\gamma$ in males, averaging $\bar{\beta} = (1+\beta)/2$ across the sexes. Social partners' breeding values exhibit correlation $r_{mf}$. Each generation of selection multiplies a gene copy's association with its selective background by the retention factor $Z$ (Appendix~\ref{app:B_Z}). Except in Section~\ref{app:C_mult}, the following derivations employ the small-variance approximation ($V_A \ll 1$).

\subsection{Long-term contributions and \texorpdfstring{$\kappa$}{kappa}}\label{app:C_kappa}
Consider a neutral gene copy in individual $X$ with social partner $X'$. The copy's expected number of transmitted descendants is proportional to $1 + u_X$. In a descendant carrier with expected breeding value $h$, the partner's expected breeding value is $r_{mf}h$, contributing to the carrier's advantage with weight $1$ in female nests and $1 - \alpha$ in male nests. The carrier's expected advantage is thus $\tilde{\beta}\,h$, where
\begin{equation}
    \tilde{\beta} = \bar{\beta} + \tfrac12 r_{mf}(2 - \alpha).
\end{equation}
Offspring that inherit the copy receive half of $h$ and half of the other parent's expected breeding value. The other parent's value is $r_{mf}h$ when that parent is the social partner, with probability $1 - \alpha$, and zero when it is an extra-pair parent. Because selection then multiplies the association by $Z$, expected breeding values decline geometrically, $h_{t+1} = \theta h_t$ with $\theta = (Z/2)[1 + (1-\alpha)r_{mf}]$\claude{, the fraction of a carrier's expected breeding value that the next carrier keeps (half by segregation, raised by a correlated partner, times the retention factor)}. In the first generation the partner's breeding value is known, $h_1 = (Z/2)[a_X + (1-\alpha)a_{X'}]$. Summing $\tilde{\beta}\,h_t$ over $t \ge 1$ gives the cumulative advantage of the copy, $A_X = u_X + \Psi[a_X + (1-\alpha)a_{X'}]$ with $\Psi = \tilde{\beta}(Z/2)/(1-\theta)$\claude{, the descendants' part of the cumulative advantage, so that $1 + \Psi = Q$ under strict monogamy and, at $r_{mf} = 0$, under random mating}:
\begin{equation}
    A_f = (1+\Psi)\,a_f + \left[1 + \Psi(1-\alpha)\right] a_{m'}, \qquad
    A_m = (\beta+\Psi)\,a_m + (1+\Psi)(1-\alpha)\,a_{f'}. \label{eq:C_L}
\end{equation}
For small $V_A$, $1 + A_X$ approximates the expected long-term contribution $R$ (Equation~\eqref{eq:B_R}), representing the regression of contribution on ancestral selective advantages~\cite[Equation~6b]{woolliams1999}. In each sex, $\mathbb{E}[R^2] = 1 + \mathrm{Var}(A)$, yielding $N_{e,X}/N = 1/[1 + \mathrm{Var}(A_X)]$. Combining sexes via Santiago and Caballero's~\cite{santiago1995} formula $1/N_e = \frac{1}{4N_{e,m}} + \frac{1}{4N_{e,f}}$ (following Crow and Denniston~\cite{crow1988}) at an equal sex ratio makes $N/N_e$ the mean of $1 + \mathrm{Var}(A)$ across sexes:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{N_e}{N} = \frac{1}{1 + \tfrac{1}{2}\left[\mathrm{Var}(A_f) + \mathrm{Var}(A_m)\right]} \equiv \frac{1}{1 + 4\kappa V_A}. \label{eq:kappa_def}
\end{equation}
Here $4\kappa V_A$ is the sex-averaged variance of long-term contributions, and $\kappa$ scales this variance relative to its random-mating baseline ($Z = 1$, $r_{mf} = 0$, $4V_A$). For a weighted sum $\xi_1 a + \xi_2 a'$ of partner breeding values with correlation $r_{mf}$, the variance is $V_A\,\sigma^2(\xi_1, \xi_2) = V_A(\xi_1^2 + \xi_2^2 + 2r_{mf}\xi_1\xi_2)$, yielding
\begin{equation}
    \kappa = \frac{\sigma^2\!\left(1+\Psi,\ 1+\Psi(1-\alpha)\right) + \sigma^2\!\left(\beta+\Psi,\ (1-\alpha)(1+\Psi)\right)}{8}. \label{eq:C_kappa_general}
\end{equation}

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{$\kappa$}{kappa} under Models A and B}\label{app:C_limits}
When $Z = 1$ and $r_{mf} = 0$, $\theta = 1/2$ and $\Psi = \bar{\beta}$. Model B ($\beta = \bar{\beta} = 1$) reduces Equation~\eqref{eq:C_kappa_general} to
\begin{equation}
    \kappa_B = 2 - \tfrac32\alpha + \tfrac58\alpha^2. \label{eq:kappaB}
\end{equation}
For Model A ($\beta = 1 - \alpha$, $\bar{\beta} = 1 - \alpha/2$),
\begin{equation}
    \kappa_A(\alpha) = \frac{(4-\alpha)^2\left[1+(1-\alpha)^2\right] + (4-3\alpha+\alpha^2)^2 + (4-3\alpha)^2}{32}.
\end{equation}
As $\alpha$ increases to $1$, $\kappa_B$ decreases monotonically to $9/8$ (staying above $1$ because social males retain productivity contributions), whereas $\kappa_A$ drops to $7/16$ and falls below $1$ once $\alpha > 0.44$ (Figure~\ref{fig:S_kappa}).

Under strict monogamy ($\alpha = 0$), both models yield $1 + \Psi = \frac{2}{2 - Z(1 + r_{mf})} = Q$, giving $A_f = Q(a_f + a_{m'})$ and recovering the pair-mated model of Santiago and Caballero~\cite{santiago1995} with $\kappa = 2$ at $Z = 1, r_{mf} = 0$. Under random mating, $u = a$ in both sexes ($\tilde{\beta} = 1$), yielding $A = \frac{a}{1-\theta} = Q\,a$ and $\kappa = Q^2/4$ ($1$ at $Z = 1, r_{mf} = 0$). Positive assortative mating ($r_{mf} > 0$) extends association persistence via $\theta$ to elevate $\kappa$; we set $r_{mf} = 0$ in the absence of empirical estimates. Under random mating and Model B, carrier weights equal $1$ in both sexes, preserving the single-sex retention factor $Z = 1$ (Gaussian) or $Z_k = 1 - s_k$ (deleterious classes; Equation~\eqref{eq:Zclass}).

\subsection{Own, partner, and cis components}\label{app:C_split}
Evaluating linked selection requires partitioning $\kappa$ into components attributable to the carrier versus its social partner (the divergence between ancestor-centered contributions and gene-centered backgrounds; \autoref{sec:unlinked}). Because neither the homologous (\emph{trans}) haplotype nor a social partner's breeding value co-segregates with the focal gene copy, the individual ancestor's long-term contribution no longer governs drift at linked sites. \claude{Because meiosis separates the homologous chromosomes every generation, a neutral allele remains linked only to the selected alleles in \emph{cis} on its own haplotype}~\cite{buffaloKern2024}, which carries half the chromosome's diploid variance ($v_h = v_i/2$). Similarly, only the carrier's own breeding value\claude{, not its mate's,} acts in \emph{cis}. Setting $r_{mf} = 0$, $\mathrm{Var}(A_f) + \mathrm{Var}(A_m)$ separates into carrier and partner variances:
\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
    \kappa = \kappa_\text{own} + \kappa_\text{partner}, \\
    \kappa_\text{own} = \frac{(1+\Psi)^2 + (\beta+\Psi)^2}{8}, \qquad
    \kappa_\text{partner} = \frac{\left[1+\Psi(1-\alpha)\right]^2 + (1-\alpha)^2(1+\Psi)^2}{8}.
\end{gathered}
\label{eq:kappa_split}
\end{equation}
The partner's genes are never on the focal copy's haplotype, so the partner component is unlinked. The share of the own component that lies on the focal haplotype, $v_h$, is assigned to the linked term. In the linked term, a selected locus keeps its association with the copy with probability $\rho = (1-r)Z$ per generation. When every carrier has weight 1, the cumulative advantage per unit of the locus's effect is the linked kernel $1/(1-\rho) = 1 + \chi$ of \autoref{sec:linkage}, with $\chi = \rho/(1-\rho)$. With the carrier's own weight $\beta_X$ (1 in females, $\beta$ in males) and later carriers' average weight $\bar{\beta}$, the cumulative advantage is $\beta_X + \bar{\beta}\chi$ instead. The square of this weighted kernel relative to the square of the unweighted one, averaged over the sexes, is
\begin{equation}
    \kappa_\text{cis}(\rho) = \frac{\left[1 + \bar{\beta}\chi\right]^2 + \left[\beta + \bar{\beta}\chi\right]^2}{2(1+\chi)^2},
\end{equation}
which decreases from $(1+\beta^2)/2$ as $\rho \to 0$ to $\bar{\beta}^2$ under tight linkage. Under random mating, and under Model B, $\beta = 1$, so $\kappa_\text{own} = \kappa_\text{cis} = 1$ and $\kappa_\text{partner} = \kappa - 1$. The remainder of the genome \claude{(the homologous haplotype and all other chromosomes)} assorts independently of the focal neutral site\claude{, so Equation}~\eqref{total_red} \claude{uses this split, applying} $\kappa_\text{cis}$ to the linked term and $Q^2(\kappa V_A - \kappa_\text{own}v_h)$ to the exponent of the unlinked background, with $Q = 2/(2-Z)$ at the same retention factor as the linked term. \claude{Because the \emph{cis} haplotype variance $v_h$ moves from the unlinked to the linked term, no variance is counted twice. The two terms combine additively in the exponent if selected loci are distributed independently along the genetic map, so that their effects multiply}~\cite{hudson1995,santiago2016}\claude{, and fitness is multiplicative across loci}~\cite[Supplementary Equations~28--29]{buffaloKern2024}; \cite[cf.][]{santiago1998}\claude{. Across a DFE, $\overline{Q^2}_i$ and $Q^2$ are replaced by their averages over the variance weights $\nu_k$, and the multiplicative bound replaces $\kappa$ by $\kappa_\text{max}$ (Appendix}~\ref{app:omega}\claude{ gives the small-variance form).}

\subsection{The multiplicative model with pair bonds}\label{app:C_mult}
Under Model B with multiplicative fitness ($w \propto e^{z}$), a gene copy's long-term contribution flows through within-pair and extra-pair routes. In females, productivity is $e^{z_f + z_{m'}}$, with a fraction $1 - \alpha$ of offspring sired by $m'$ and $\alpha$ by extra-pair sires. In males, contributions arise from within-nest and extra-pair offspring proportional to $e^{z_m}$. Following Appendix~\ref{app:B_R}, expected contributions evolve as
\begin{equation}
    R_f \propto (1-\alpha)\,e^{2a_f + 2a_{m'}} + \alpha \mathcal{E}_f\,e^{2a_f + a_{m'}}, \qquad
    R_m \propto (1-\alpha)\,e^{2a_m + 2a_{f'}} + \alpha \mathcal{E}_m\,e^{2a_m},
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{E}_f$ and $\mathcal{E}_m$ absorb expectations over outside individuals and equal $1$ at $V_A = 0$. Letting $\mathbf{a} = (a_X, a_{X'})$ denote independent carrier and partner deviations, contributions take the mixture form $R = \sum_l \mathcal{C}_l e^{\boldsymbol{\lambda}_l\cdot\mathbf{a}}$, with exponent vectors $\boldsymbol{\lambda} \in \{(2,2), (2,1)\}$ in females and $\{(2,2), (2,0)\}$ in males, and coefficients $\mathcal{C}_1 = 1 - \alpha$, $\mathcal{C}_2 = \alpha\mathcal{E}_X$. Using the Gaussian moment-generating function $\mathbb{E}[e^{\boldsymbol{\lambda}\cdot\mathbf{a}}] = e^{V_A|\boldsymbol{\lambda}|^2/2}$,
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\mathbb{E}[R^2]}{\mathbb{E}[R]^2} = \sum_{l,l'} \omega_l \omega_{l'}\, e^{V_A\,\boldsymbol{\lambda}_l\cdot\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{l'}}, \qquad
    \omega_l = \frac{\mathcal{C}_l\, e^{V_A|\boldsymbol{\lambda}_l|^2/2}}{\sum_{l'} \mathcal{C}_{l'}\, e^{V_A|\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{l'}|^2/2}}, \label{eq:C_moments}
\end{equation}
where $\omega_l$ is route $l$'s share of expected long-term contribution. Averaging over sexes per Equation~\eqref{eq:B_ratio} yields $N_e/N$. Because all dot products $\boldsymbol{\lambda}_l\cdot\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{l'} \le 8$, the ratio in Equation~\eqref{eq:C_moments} is bounded above by $e^{8V_A}$ in either sex. Conversely, Jensen's inequality bounds the sex mean below by $\exp(V_A|\bar{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}_X|^2)$. At baseline weights $(\omega_1, \omega_2) = (1-\alpha, \alpha)$, $\tfrac{1}{2}(|\bar{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}_f|^2 + |\bar{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}_m|^2) = 8 - 6\alpha + \tfrac{5}{2}\alpha^2 = 4\kappa_B$. Consequently, provided extra-pair routes carry no more than proportional share $\alpha$,
\begin{equation}
    e^{-8V_A} \le \frac{N_e}{N} \le e^{-4\kappa_B V_A}. \label{eq:C_mult_pair}
\end{equation}
This bound holds for extra-pair multipliers $\mathcal{E}_f, \mathcal{E}_m$ up to $e^{2V_A}$. At $\alpha = 0$, $\kappa_B = 2$ and both bounds collapse to Weissman and Barton's~\cite{weissman2012} strict-monogamy result ($e^{-8V_A}$; Equation~\eqref{eq:B_mono}). For small $V_A$, the upper bound recovers $1/(1 + 4\kappa_B V_A)$, whereas for larger $V_A$ the within-pair route dominates $\mathbb{E}[R^2]$, shifting the exact ratio toward the lower bound.
