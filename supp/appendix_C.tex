\section{Linked Interference along a Chromosome}\label{app:chromosapp}
This appendix evaluates the chromosome-wide interference $\overline{Q^2}_i$ of Equation~\eqref{eq:haldane_avg}. It gives the closed form, the limits, the linear map as a lower bound, the bound $\Omega_i \le 1$ on the penalty of Equation~\eqref{linked_penalty}, and the effect of placing selected variance unevenly. Consider one effect class with retention $Z$ (Equation~\eqref{eq:Z}); under a distribution of fitness effects each expression is averaged over classes with the weights $\nu_k$ of \autoref{sec:linkage}. A chromosome has map length $M$ Morgans, and two sites $\ell$ Morgans apart recombine with probability $r(\ell)$. Selected variance and neutral sites are spread uniformly along the map until Appendix~\ref{app:C_spatial}. We drop the chromosome index. $\overline{Q^2}(y)$ is the interference at a neutral site at position $y$, averaged over selected loci. $\overline{Q^2}_\text{H}$ and $\overline{Q^2}_\text{L}$ are its averages over the chromosome under the Haldane and linear maps; $\overline{Q^2}_\text{H}$ is the $\overline{Q^2}_i$ of the main text. $Q^2 = [2/(2-Z)]^2$ denotes only the unlinked value (Equation~\eqref{eq:Q}).

\subsection{The kernel and its average}\label{app:C_kernel}
An association between the neutral site and a selected locus $\ell$ Morgans away survives one generation with probability $(1 - r(\ell))Z$: recombination breaks it with probability $r(\ell)$, and selection retains a fraction $Z$. Summed over generations, the cumulative advantage that the locus confers on the neutral site, per unit of its effect, is
\begin{equation}
    \sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\left[(1 - r(\ell))\,Z\right]^t = \frac{1}{1-(1-r(\ell))Z}.
\end{equation}
This is the cumulative factor of Santiago and Caballero (2016) and Buffalo and Kern (2024, Equation 3). It is the counterpart of $Q$ for the chromosome segment that carries the neutral site: the advantage counted over every generation in which the segment keeps the selected locus. It appears squared in the variance of the segment's long-term contributions, as $Q$ does in Equation~\eqref{eq:A_first}, so a locus at distance $\ell$ contributes $f(\ell) = [1 - (1 - r(\ell))Z]^{-2}$. With $F(D) = \int_0^D f(\ell)\,d\ell$, the profile and its chromosome average are
\begin{gather}
    \overline{Q^2}(y) = \frac{1}{M}\int_0^M f(\vert x-y\vert)\,dx = \frac{1}{M}\left[F(y) + F(M-y)\right], \label{eq:C_profile} \\
    \frac{1}{M}\int_0^M \overline{Q^2}(y)\,dy = \frac{2}{M^2}\int_0^M (M-\ell)\,f(\ell)\,d\ell = \frac{2}{M^2}\int_0^M F(D)\,dD, \label{eq:C_haldane_avg}
\end{gather}
where the middle form weights each distance by the number of pairs of sites at that distance. The split of the profile at $y$ follows Santiago and Caballero (2016) and Buffalo and Kern (2024, Supplementary Equation 32). Under Haldane's map, $r(\ell) = \tfrac12(1 - e^{-2\ell})$, Equation~\eqref{eq:C_haldane_avg} is Equation~\eqref{eq:haldane_avg}.

\subsection{Closed form under the Haldane map}\label{app:C_closed}
Under Haldane's map the survival probability is $\tfrac{Z}{2}(1 + e^{-2\ell})$, so
\begin{equation}
    f_\text{H}(\ell) = \left[\Gamma - \eta\, e^{-2\ell}\right]^{-2}, \qquad \Gamma = 1 - \tfrac{Z}{2}, \quad \eta = \tfrac{Z}{2}.
\end{equation}
The substitution $\tau = e^{-2\ell}$ gives the arm integral in elementary functions,
\begin{equation}
    F_\text{H}(D) = \frac{1}{2}\left\{\frac{2D + \ln\!\left[(\Gamma - \eta e^{-2D})/(1-Z)\right]}{\Gamma^2} + \frac{1}{\Gamma}\left[\frac{1}{1-Z} - \frac{1}{\Gamma - \eta e^{-2D}}\right]\right\}.
\label{eq:C_arm}
\end{equation}
Integrating once more, with $\int_0^M \ln(\Gamma - \eta e^{-2D})\,dD = M\ln \Gamma + \frac{1}{2}\left[\mathrm{Li}_2(\epsilon e^{-2M}) - \mathrm{Li}_2(\epsilon)\right]$ and $\epsilon = \eta/\Gamma$, gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
    \overline{Q^2}_\text{H} = \frac{1}{M^2}\Bigg\{ &\frac{M^2 - M\ln(1-Z) + M\ln \Gamma + \tfrac{1}{2}\left[\mathrm{Li}_2(\epsilon e^{-2M}) - \mathrm{Li}_2(\epsilon)\right]}{\Gamma^2} \\
    &+ \frac{1}{\Gamma}\left[\frac{M}{1-Z} - \frac{2M + \ln\!\left[(\Gamma-\eta e^{-2M})/(1-Z)\right]}{2\Gamma}\right]\Bigg\},
\end{split}
\label{eq:C_closed_haldane}
\end{equation}
with $\mathrm{Li}_2$ the dilogarithm. Equation~\eqref{eq:C_closed_haldane} is the form the analysis computes (Methods). It agrees with quadrature of Equation~\eqref{eq:C_haldane_avg} to ten significant digits for $Z \in [0.5, 0.999]$ and $M \in [0.05, 50]$.

\subsection{Limits}\label{app:C_limits}
As $M \to 0$ every distance vanishes and $\overline{Q^2}_\text{H} \to (1-Z)^{-2}$, the limit of background selection without recombination. Reaching it needs the whole chromosome to lie within the decay distance of the kernel, $M \ll 1 - Z$, not merely a short map: at $Z = 0.99$ and $M = 0.5$, $\overline{Q^2}_\text{H} = 387$, against $(1-Z)^{-2} = 10^4$. At large distances $f_\text{H}(\ell) \to \Gamma^{-2} = Q^2$, the unlinked value at the same retention, but the approach is slow:
\begin{equation}
    \overline{Q^2}_\text{H} = Q^2 + \frac{2}{M}\int_0^\infty \left[f_\text{H}(\ell) - Q^2\right]d\ell + O(M^{-2}).
\end{equation}
The integral is close to $1/(1-Z)$: 10.3 at $Z = 0.9$ and 105 at $Z = 0.99$. Free recombination is therefore reached only when $M \gg 1/(1-Z)$. At $Z = 0.99$ and $M = 10$, $\overline{Q^2}_\text{H} = 24.8$, against $Q^2 = 3.9$. Real chromosomes lie between the two limits, near $\overline{Q^2}_\text{H} \approx 2/[M(1-Z)]$ (Santiago and Caballero 1998, Equation 8). Short chromosomes do not approach the first limit either: with one obligate crossover per bivalent, avian microchromosomes have map lengths of about 0.5 M or more. Table~\ref{tab:linked_limits} gives the limits for a class of deleterious mutations purged at rate $s$.

\subsection{The linear map as a lower bound}\label{app:C_linear}
The linear map, $r(\ell) = \ell$, gives $f_\text{L}(\ell) = (1 - Z + \ell Z)^{-2}$ and $F_\text{L}(D) = D/[(1-Z)(1-Z+DZ)]$. Its mid-chromosome profile is Buffalo and Kern's (2024) Supplementary Equation 33. Equation~\eqref{eq:C_haldane_avg} gives
\begin{equation}
    \overline{Q^2}_\text{L} = \frac{2}{M^2(1-Z)}\int_0^M \frac{y}{1-Z+yZ}\,dy = \frac{2}{MZ(1-Z)} - \frac{2}{M^2Z^2}\ln\!\left(1+\frac{MZ}{1-Z}\right).
\label{eq:closed_form}
\end{equation}
At large $M$ its leading term is $2/[MZ(1-Z)]$, the intermediate regime of Appendix~\ref{app:C_limits}. At small $M$, $\overline{Q^2}_\text{L} = (1-Z)^{-2} - \frac{2}{3}MZ(1-Z)^{-3} + O(M^2)$, and because $r_\text{H}(\ell) = \ell + O(\ell^2)$ the Haldane form agrees to that order. As $M \to \infty$, however, $\overline{Q^2}_\text{L} \to 0$ rather than $Q^2$: the linear map lets $r$ exceed $\tfrac12$, and beyond $\ell = 1$ its survival probability is negative.

Because $e^{-2\ell} > 1 - 2\ell$, the Haldane recombination fraction is below the linear one at every distance, $r_\text{H}(\ell) < \ell$. Up to $\ell = 1$ the linear map therefore underestimates the survival of every association, and beyond it $f_\text{L} < 1 < Q^2 \le f_\text{H}$. So $f_\text{L} < f_\text{H}$ at every distance for $Z > 0$, and
\begin{equation}
    \overline{Q^2}_\text{L} < \overline{Q^2}_\text{H} \qquad \text{for all } M > 0.
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:closed_form} is a lower bound on interference, and hence on the reduction in $N_e$. The gap grows with $M$ and with $1-Z$. $\overline{Q^2}_\text{L}/\overline{Q^2}_\text{H}$ is 0.99, 0.95 and 0.91 at $M = 0.1$, 1 and 3 Morgans for $Z = 0.99$, and 0.99, 0.85 and 0.68 for $Z = 0.9$. The main text uses the Haldane closed form, Equation~\eqref{eq:C_closed_haldane}.

\subsection{The linkage penalty does not exceed one}\label{app:omega}
Because $f_\text{H}$ decreases from $(1-Z)^{-2}$ to $Q^2$, every profile satisfies $\overline{Q^2}(y) \ge Q^2$. So does the chromosome average under any placement of selected variance (Appendix~\ref{app:C_spatial}). Write
\begin{equation}
    \Lambda_\text{L} = \kappa_\text{cis}\overline{Q^2}_iv_h, \qquad \Lambda_\text{own} = \kappa_\text{own}Q^2v_h, \qquad \Lambda_\text{U} = Q^2\kappa V_A.
\end{equation}
In the primary model $\kappa_\text{cis} = \kappa_\text{own}$ (Appendix~\ref{app:B_split}), so $\Lambda_\text{L} \ge \Lambda_\text{own}$; and $\Lambda_\text{U} \ge \Lambda_\text{own}$, because $v_h \le V_A$ and $\kappa \ge \kappa_\text{own}$. Under the multiplicative form of Equation~\eqref{total_red}, $\Omega_i = e^{-(\Lambda_\text{L} - \Lambda_\text{own})} \le 1$ at once. Equality holds on a freely recombining chromosome, where $\overline{Q^2}_i \to Q^2$. With the small-variance global factor $1/(1 + \Lambda_\text{U} - \Lambda_\text{own})$ the bound also holds, because $e^{-\Lambda} \le 1/(1+\Lambda)$:
\begin{equation}
    \Omega_i = e^{-\Lambda_\text{L}}\,\frac{1+\Lambda_\text{U}}{1+\Lambda_\text{U}-\Lambda_\text{own}} \le \frac{1+\Lambda_\text{U}}{(1+\Lambda_\text{own})(1+\Lambda_\text{U}-\Lambda_\text{own})} \le 1.
\end{equation}
The last step holds because $(1+\Lambda_\text{own})(1+\Lambda_\text{U}-\Lambda_\text{own}) - (1+\Lambda_\text{U}) = \Lambda_\text{own}(\Lambda_\text{U}-\Lambda_\text{own}) \ge 0$. On a freely recombining chromosome this form gives $\Omega_i = 1 - \Lambda_\text{own}\Lambda_\text{U}/(1 + \Lambda_\text{U}) + O(\Lambda_\text{own}^2)$. That departure, of order $v_hV_A$, reflects only the difference between the exponential and reciprocal forms of the same variance. Under a distribution of fitness effects the argument applies to each effect class.

\subsection{Non-uniform placement of selected variance}\label{app:C_spatial}
The genetic architecture of fitness in wild populations is unresolved (Barton and Keightley 2002), so the uniform density is a baseline. Let $g(x) \ge 0$, with $\int_0^M g(x)\,dx = 1$, be the density of selected variance along the chromosome. With neutral sites still uniform, the chromosome average is
\begin{equation}
    \overline{Q^2}_\text{H}[g] = \frac{1}{M}\int_0^M\!\!\int_0^M g(x)\, f_\text{H}(\vert x-y\vert)\,dx\,dy = \int_0^M g(x)\,\overline{Q^2}(x)\,dx, \label{general_g}
\end{equation}
because $f_\text{H}$ depends only on $\vert x - y\vert$, so the inner average over $y$ is the uniform profile at $x$ (Equation~\eqref{eq:C_profile}). With $g = 1/M$ it recovers $\overline{Q^2}_\text{H}$; for $J$ loci of equal effect at $x_1, \ldots, x_J$ it is the mean of $\overline{Q^2}(x_j)$. Since $\overline{Q^2}(x) \ge Q^2$ everywhere, $\Omega_i \le 1$ holds for every $g$. The profile is strictly concave, because its second derivative, $[f_\text{H}'(x) + f_\text{H}'(M-x)]/M$, is negative. It is largest at the midpoint and smallest at the telomeres: a central locus interferes across two chromosome arms, a terminal one across one.

The reduction depends on the expectation of the convex function $\exp(-\kappa_\text{cis} v_h \overline{Q^2})$. For a single locus at a uniformly distributed position $x_0$, Jensen's inequality gives
\begin{equation}
    \mathbb{E}_{x_0}\left[\exp\left(-\kappa_\text{cis} v_h \overline{Q^2}(x_0)\right)\right] > \exp\left(-\kappa_\text{cis} v_h \overline{Q^2}_\text{H}\right),
\end{equation}
so a localized locus retains, on average, more neutral diversity than the same variance spread uniformly. For $J$ independently placed loci the gap is, to second order,
\begin{equation}
    \mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(-\kappa_\text{cis} v_h \overline{Q^2}_\text{H}[g]\right)\right] - \exp\left(-\kappa_\text{cis} v_h \overline{Q^2}_\text{H}\right) \approx \frac{(\kappa_\text{cis} v_h)^2}{2J}\exp\left(-\kappa_\text{cis} v_h \overline{Q^2}_\text{H}\right)\mathrm{Var}_x\left[\overline{Q^2}(x)\right].
\end{equation}
Under random placement the uniform density therefore gives the largest expected reduction, so it does not understate the effect of linkage. This ordering assumes that loci are placed independently of local recombination rate. If selected alleles are concentrated where recombination is suppressed, as in pericentromeric regions or inversions (Kirkpatrick and Barton 2006; Yeaman 2013), their density in map distance rises. $\overline{Q^2}(x_j)$ then exceeds $\overline{Q^2}_\text{H}$, and the gap can close or reverse.
