\section{Effective size with separate sexes, pair bonds and extra-pair paternity}\label{app:matingapp}
This appendix derives the mating-system scalar $\kappa$ of Equations~\eqref{unlinked_mating} and \eqref{eq:kappaB}, its split into the components used in Equation~\eqref{total_red}, and the multiplicative-model bracket of Equation~\eqref{eq:B_mult_pair}. It applies the expected long-term contributions of Woolliams, Bijma, and Villanueva~\cite{woolliams1999} and the two-sex combination of Santiago and Caballero~\cite{santiago1995}. 

Consider a diploid population with discrete generations, $N$ breeding adults, and an equal sex ratio. Each individual possesses an additive breeding value $a$ for relative lifetime breeding success, with variance $\mathrm{Var}(a) = V_A$ assumed equal in both sexes. For small advantages, $a = z - \bar{z}$ (Appendix~\ref{app:unlinkedapp}). Bonnet et al.~\cite{bonnet2022} estimate this single variance from an animal model incorporating both sexes with sex fitted as a fixed effect.

Under random mating, a parent's relative family size is $1 + a$. Under social monogamy, the nest serves as the unit of productivity. A female paired with social partner $m'$ has relative family size $1 + u_f = 1 + a_f + a_{m'}$. Extra-pair paternity at rate $\alpha$ moves paternity out of the nest without altering its overall productivity, so a male paired with social partner $f'$ achieves relative success
\begin{equation}
    u_m = (1-\alpha)(a_m + a_{f'}) + \alpha\gamma\, a_m,
\end{equation}
where $\gamma$ indexes the dependence of extra-pair siring success on the male's own breeding value. Model A sets $\gamma = 0$ (extra-pair siring as a random lottery), whereas Model B sets $\gamma = 1$ (extra-pair gains exactly offset within-pair losses). Model B is the unique parameterization in which a male's breeding value contributes to his total fitness with the same average weight as a female's across all $\alpha$, satisfying the single-variance assumption of the animal model. Model B is therefore adopted for all primary results. 

A carrier's own breeding value thus contributes to its advantage with weight $1$ in females and $\beta = 1 - \alpha + \alpha\gamma$ in males, averaging $\bar{\beta} = (1+\beta)/2$ across the sexes. Social partners' breeding values exhibit correlation $r_{mf}$. Each generation of selection multiplies a gene copy's association with its selective background by the retention factor $Z$ (Appendix~\ref{app:A_Z}). Except in Section~\ref{app:B_mult}, the following derivations employ the small-variance approximation ($V_A \ll 1$).

\subsection{Long-term contributions and \texorpdfstring{$\kappa$}{kappa}}\label{app:B_kappa}
Consider a neutral gene copy in individual $X$ with social partner $X'$. The copy's expected number of transmitted descendants is proportional to $1 + u_X$. In a descendant carrier with expected breeding value $h$, the partner's expected breeding value is $r_{mf}h$, contributing to the carrier's advantage with weight $1$ in female nests and $1 - \alpha$ in male nests. The carrier's expected advantage is thus $\tilde{\beta}\,h$, where
\begin{equation}
    \tilde{\beta} = \bar{\beta} + \tfrac12 r_{mf}(2 - \alpha).
\end{equation}
Offspring inheriting the copy receive half of $h$ and half of the other parent's expected breeding value ($r_{mf}h$ when the social partner, with probability $1 - \alpha$, and zero for an extra-pair sire). Multiplying the gene--background association by $Z$ each generation yields geometric decay, $h_{t+1} = \theta h_t$, with $\theta = \frac{Z}{2}[1 + (1-\alpha)r_{mf}]$. In the first generation, the partner's value is fixed at $h_1 = \frac{Z}{2}[a_X + (1-\alpha)a_{X'}]$. Summing $\tilde{\beta}\,h_t$ over $t \ge 1$ gives the cumulative advantage $A_X = u_X + \Psi[a_X + (1-\alpha)a_{X'}]$, with $\Psi = \frac{\tilde{\beta} Z / 2}{1 - \theta}$:
\begin{equation}
    A_f = (1+\Psi)\,a_f + \left[1 + \Psi(1-\alpha)\right] a_{m'}, \qquad
    A_m = (\beta+\Psi)\,a_m + (1+\Psi)(1-\alpha)\,a_{f'}. \label{eq:B_L}
\end{equation}
For small $V_A$, $1 + A_X$ approximates the expected long-term contribution $R$ (Equation~\eqref{eq:A_R}), representing the regression of contribution on ancestral selective advantages~\cite[Equation~6b]{woolliams1999}. In each sex, $\mathbb{E}[R^2] = 1 + \mathrm{Var}(A)$, yielding $N_{e,X}/N = 1/[1 + \mathrm{Var}(A_X)]$. Combining sexes via Santiago and Caballero's~\cite{santiago1995} formula $1/N_e = \frac{1}{4N_{e,m}} + \frac{1}{4N_{e,f}}$ (following Crow and Denniston~\cite{crow1988}) at an equal sex ratio makes $N/N_e$ the mean of $1 + \mathrm{Var}(A)$ across sexes:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{N_e}{N} = \frac{1}{1 + \tfrac{1}{2}\left[\mathrm{Var}(A_f) + \mathrm{Var}(A_m)\right]} \equiv \frac{1}{1 + 4\kappa V_A}. \label{eq:kappa_def}
\end{equation}
Here $4\kappa V_A$ is the sex-averaged variance of long-term contributions, and $\kappa$ scales this variance relative to its random-mating baseline ($Z = 1$, $r_{mf} = 0$, $4V_A$). For a weighted sum $\xi_1 a + \xi_2 a'$ of partner breeding values with correlation $r_{mf}$, the variance is $V_A\,\sigma^2(\xi_1, \xi_2) = V_A(\xi_1^2 + \xi_2^2 + 2r_{mf}\xi_1\xi_2)$, yielding
\begin{equation}
    \kappa = \frac{\sigma^2\!\left(1+\Psi,\ 1+\Psi(1-\alpha)\right) + \sigma^2\!\left(\beta+\Psi,\ (1-\alpha)(1+\Psi)\right)}{8}. \label{eq:B_kappa_general}
\end{equation}

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{$\kappa$}{kappa} under Models A and B}\label{app:B_limits}
When $Z = 1$ and $r_{mf} = 0$, $\theta = 1/2$ and $\Psi = \bar{\beta}$. Model B ($\beta = \bar{\beta} = 1$) reduces Equation~\eqref{eq:B_kappa_general} to Equation~\eqref{eq:kappaB}:
\begin{equation}
    \kappa_B = 2 - \tfrac32\alpha + \tfrac58\alpha^2.
\end{equation}
For Model A ($\beta = 1 - \alpha$, $\bar{\beta} = 1 - \alpha/2$),
\begin{equation}
    \kappa_A(\alpha) = \frac{(4-\alpha)^2\left[1+(1-\alpha)^2\right] + (4-3\alpha+\alpha^2)^2 + (4-3\alpha)^2}{32}.
\end{equation}
As $\alpha$ increases to $1$, $\kappa_B$ decreases monotonically to $9/8$ (staying above $1$ because social males retain productivity contributions), whereas $\kappa_A$ drops to $7/16$ and falls below $1$ once $\alpha > 0.44$ (Figure~\ref{fig:S_kappa}).

Under strict monogamy ($\alpha = 0$), both models yield $1 + \Psi = \frac{2}{2 - Z(1 + r_{mf})} = Q$, giving $A_f = Q(a_f + a_{m'})$ and recovering the pair-mated model of Santiago and Caballero~\cite{santiago1995} with $\kappa = 2$ at $Z = 1, r_{mf} = 0$. Under random mating, $u = a$ in both sexes ($\tilde{\beta} = 1$), yielding $A = \frac{a}{1-\theta} = Q\,a$ and $\kappa = Q^2/4$ ($1$ at $Z = 1, r_{mf} = 0$). Positive assortative mating ($r_{mf} > 0$) extends association persistence via $\theta$ to elevate $\kappa$; we set $r_{mf} = 0$ in the absence of empirical estimates. Under random mating and Model B, carrier weights equal $1$ in both sexes, preserving the single-sex retention factor $Z = 1$ (Gaussian) or $Z_k = 1 - s_k$ (deleterious classes; Equation~\eqref{eq:Zclass}).

\subsection{Own, partner, and cis components}\label{app:B_split}
Evaluating linked selection requires partitioning $\kappa$ into components attributable to the carrier versus its social partner (the divergence between ancestor-centered contributions and gene-centered backgrounds; \autoref{sec:unlinked}). Setting $r_{mf} = 0$, $\mathrm{Var}(A_f) + \mathrm{Var}(A_m)$ separates into carrier and partner variances:
\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
    \kappa = \kappa_\text{own} + \kappa_\text{partner}, \\
    \kappa_\text{own} = \frac{(1+\Psi)^2 + (\beta+\Psi)^2}{8}, \qquad
    \kappa_\text{partner} = \frac{\left[1+\Psi(1-\alpha)\right]^2 + (1-\alpha)^2(1+\Psi)^2}{8}.
\end{gathered}
\label{eq:kappa_split}
\end{equation}
Because social partners' genes never reside on the focal gene's haplotype, $\kappa_\text{partner}$ is strictly unlinked. The fraction of $\kappa_\text{own}$ residing on the focal haplotype, $v_h$, enters the linked term. For a selected locus retaining association $\rho = (1-r)Z$ per generation, the cumulative advantage per unit effect is the linked kernel $1/(1-\rho) = 1 + \chi$ ($\chi = \rho/(1-\rho)$; \autoref{sec:linkage}). Incorporating sex-specific carrier weights ($\beta_X$ and $\bar{\beta}$), the sex-averaged squared weighted kernel relative to unweighted background is
\begin{equation}
    \kappa_\text{cis}(\rho) = \frac{\left[1 + \bar{\beta}\chi\right]^2 + \left[\beta + \bar{\beta}\chi\right]^2}{2(1+\chi)^2},
\end{equation}
which declines from $(1+\beta^2)/2$ at $\rho \to 0$ to $\bar{\beta}^2$ under tight linkage. Under random mating and Model B ($\beta = 1$), $\kappa_\text{own} = \kappa_\text{cis} = 1$ and $\kappa_\text{partner} = \kappa - 1$. Equation~\eqref{total_red} applies $\kappa_\text{cis}$ to the linked term and $Q^2(\kappa V_A - \kappa_\text{own}v_h)$ to the unlinked exponent ($Q = \frac{2}{2-Z}$).

\subsection{The multiplicative model with pair bonds}\label{app:B_mult}
Under Model B with multiplicative fitness ($w \propto e^{z}$), a gene copy's long-term contribution flows through within-pair and extra-pair routes. In females, productivity is $e^{z_f + z_{m'}}$, with a fraction $1 - \alpha$ of offspring sired by $m'$ and $\alpha$ by extra-pair sires. In males, contributions arise from within-nest and extra-pair offspring proportional to $e^{z_m}$. Following Appendix~\ref{app:A_R}, expected contributions evolve as
\begin{equation}
    R_f \propto (1-\alpha)\,e^{2a_f + 2a_{m'}} + \alpha \mathcal{E}_f\,e^{2a_f + a_{m'}}, \qquad
    R_m \propto (1-\alpha)\,e^{2a_m + 2a_{f'}} + \alpha \mathcal{E}_m\,e^{2a_m},
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{E}_f$ and $\mathcal{E}_m$ absorb expectations over outside individuals and equal $1$ at $V_A = 0$. Letting $\mathbf{a} = (a_X, a_{X'})$ denote independent carrier and partner deviations, contributions take the mixture form $R = \sum_l \mathcal{C}_l e^{\boldsymbol{\lambda}_l\cdot\mathbf{a}}$, with exponent vectors $\boldsymbol{\lambda} \in \{(2,2), (2,1)\}$ in females and $\{(2,2), (2,0)\}$ in males, and coefficients $\mathcal{C}_1 = 1 - \alpha$, $\mathcal{C}_2 = \alpha\mathcal{E}_X$. Using the Gaussian moment-generating function $\mathbb{E}[e^{\boldsymbol{\lambda}\cdot\mathbf{a}}] = e^{V_A|\boldsymbol{\lambda}|^2/2}$,
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\mathbb{E}[R^2]}{\mathbb{E}[R]^2} = \sum_{l,l'} \omega_l \omega_{l'}\, e^{V_A\,\boldsymbol{\lambda}_l\cdot\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{l'}}, \qquad
    \omega_l = \frac{\mathcal{C}_l\, e^{V_A|\boldsymbol{\lambda}_l|^2/2}}{\sum_{l'} \mathcal{C}_{l'}\, e^{V_A|\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{l'}|^2/2}}, \label{eq:B_moments}
\end{equation}
where $\omega_l$ is route $l$'s share of expected long-term contribution. Averaging over sexes per Equation~\eqref{eq:A_ratio} yields $N_e/N$. Because all dot products $\boldsymbol{\lambda}_l\cdot\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{l'} \le 8$, the ratio in Equation~\eqref{eq:B_moments} is bounded above by $e^{8V_A}$ in either sex. Conversely, Jensen's inequality bounds the sex mean below by $\exp(V_A|\bar{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}_X|^2)$. At baseline weights $(\omega_1, \omega_2) = (1-\alpha, \alpha)$, $\tfrac{1}{2}(|\bar{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}_f|^2 + |\bar{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}_m|^2) = 8 - 6\alpha + \tfrac{5}{2}\alpha^2 = 4\kappa_B$. Thus, provided extra-pair routes carry no more than proportional share $\alpha$,
\begin{equation}
    e^{-8V_A} \le \frac{N_e}{N} \le e^{-4\kappa_B V_A}. \label{eq:B_mult_pair}
\end{equation}
This bound holds for extra-pair multipliers $\mathcal{E}_f, \mathcal{E}_m$ up to $e^{2V_A}$. At $\alpha = 0$, $\kappa_B = 2$ and both bounds collapse to Weissman and Barton's~\cite{weissman2012} strict-monogamy result ($e^{-8V_A}$; Equation~\eqref{eq:A_mono}). For small $V_A$, the upper bound recovers $1/(1 + 4\kappa_B V_A)$, whereas for larger $V_A$ the within-pair route dominates $\mathbb{E}[R^2]$, shifting the exact ratio toward the lower bound.
