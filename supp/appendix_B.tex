\section{Effective size with separate sexes, pair bonds and extra-pair paternity}\label{app:matingapp}
This appendix derives the mating-system scalar $\kappa$ of Equations~\eqref{unlinked_mating} and \eqref{eq:kappaB}, its split into the components used in Equation~\eqref{total_red}, and the multiplicative-model bracket of Equation~\eqref{eq:ne_mult_pair}. It applies the expected long-term contributions of Woolliams, Bijma and Villanueva~\cite{woolliams1999} and the two-sex combination of Santiago and Caballero~\cite{santiago1995}. The population is diploid, with discrete generations, $N$ breeding adults and an equal sex ratio. Each individual has a breeding value $a$ for relative lifetime breeding success, with $\mathrm{Var}(a) = V_A$ in both sexes; for small advantages $a = z - \bar{z}$ (Appendix~A). This is the single variance that Bonnet et al.~\cite{bonnet2022} estimate, from one animal model per population with both sexes in the data and sex as a fixed effect.

Under random mating, a parent's relative family size is $1 + a$. Under social monogamy the nest is the unit of productivity. A female with social partner $m'$ has relative family size $1 + u_f$, with $u_f = a_f + a_{m'}$. Extra-pair paternity at rate $\alpha$ moves paternity out of the nest without changing its productivity, so a male with social partner $f'$ has
\begin{equation}
    u_m = (1-\alpha)(a_m + a_{f'}) + \alpha\gamma\, a_m,
\end{equation}
where $\gamma$ is the dependence of his extra-pair success on his own breeding value. Model A sets $\gamma = 0$, so that extra-pair siring is a lottery. Model B sets $\gamma = 1$, so that extra-pair gains replace exactly the within-pair loss. Model B is the only version in which a male's breeding value contributes to his own breeding success with the same weight as a female's at every $\alpha$, as a single variance for both sexes assumes, and it is used for all primary results. A carrier's own breeding value therefore contributes to its advantage with weight 1 in females and $m = 1 - \alpha + \alpha\gamma$ in males, $\bar{m} = (1+m)/2$ on average. Social partners' breeding values have correlation $r_{mf}$. Each generation of selection retains a fraction $Z$ of a gene copy's association with its selective background (Appendix~\ref{app:A_Z}). The derivation uses the small-variance approximation ($V_A \ll 1$), except in Appendix~\ref{app:B_mult}.

\subsection{Long-term contributions and \texorpdfstring{$\kappa$}{kappa}}\label{app:B_kappa}
Follow a neutral gene copy in an individual $X$ with social partner $X'$. Its expected number of transmitted copies is proportional to $1 + u_X$. Consider a later carrier with expected breeding value $h$. Its partner has expected breeding value $r_{mf}h$, which contributes to the carrier's advantage with weight 1 in a female's nest and $1 - \alpha$ in a male's. The carrier's expected advantage is therefore $\bar{c}\,h$, with
\begin{equation}
    \bar{c} = \bar{m} + \tfrac12 r_{mf}(2 - \alpha).
\end{equation}
Its carrier offspring inherit half of $h$, and half of the other parent's expected value, which is $r_{mf}h$ for the social partner, with probability $1 - \alpha$, and zero for an extra-pair parent. Selection then retains a fraction $Z$ of that association, so expected breeding values decline geometrically, $h_{t+1} = \theta h_t$ with $\theta = (Z/2)[1 + (1-\alpha)r_{mf}]$. In the first generation the partner's value is known, $h_1 = (Z/2)[a_X + (1-\alpha)a_{X'}]$. Summing $\bar{c}\,h_t$ over $t \ge 1$ gives the cumulative advantage of the copy, $L_X = u_X + \Psi[a_X + (1-\alpha)a_{X'}]$ with $\Psi = \bar{c}(Z/2)/(1-\theta)$:
\begin{equation}
    L_f = (1+\Psi)\,a_f + \left[1 + \Psi(1-\alpha)\right] a_{m'}, \qquad
    L_m = (m+\Psi)\,a_m + (1+\Psi)(1-\alpha)\,a_{f'}. \label{eq:B_L}
\end{equation}
For small $V_A$, $1 + L_X$ is the expected long-term contribution $R$ of $X$ (Equation~\eqref{eq:A_R}). $L_X$ is the regression of that contribution on the selective advantages of $X$~\cite[Equation~6b]{woolliams1999}. In each sex heritable differences raise the rate of drift by the factor $1 + \mathrm{Var}(L)$ (Equation~\eqref{eq:A_ratio} for small $V_A$). Santiago and Caballero~\cite{santiago1995} combine the sexes as $1/N_e = 1/(4N_{e,m}) + 1/(4N_{e,f})$ (after Crow and Denniston~\cite{crow1988}), which at an equal sex ratio is a mean over the sexes:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{N_e}{N} = \frac{1}{1 + \tfrac{1}{2}\left[\mathrm{Var}(L_f) + \mathrm{Var}(L_m)\right]} \equiv \frac{1}{1 + 4\kappa V_A}. \label{eq:kappa_def}
\end{equation}
This is Equation~\eqref{unlinked_mating}. Partners' values correlate $r_{mf}$, so a weighted sum $\xi_1 a + \xi_2 a'$ has variance $V_A\,v(\xi_1, \xi_2)$, with $v(\xi_1, \xi_2) = \xi_1^2 + \xi_2^2 + 2r_{mf}\xi_1\xi_2$, and
\begin{equation}
    \kappa = \frac{v\!\left(1+\Psi,\ 1+\Psi(1-\alpha)\right) + v\!\left(m+\Psi,\ (1-\alpha)(1+\Psi)\right)}{8}. \label{eq:B_kappa_general}
\end{equation}

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{$\kappa$}{kappa} under Models A and B}\label{app:B_limits}
At $Z = 1$ and $r_{mf} = 0$, $\theta = 1/2$ and $\Psi = \bar{m}$. Model B ($m = \bar{m} = 1$) then gives Equation~\eqref{eq:kappaB}, $\kappa_B = 2 - \tfrac32\alpha + \tfrac58\alpha^2$. Model A ($m = 1 - \alpha$, $\bar{m} = 1 - \alpha/2$) gives
\begin{equation}
    \kappa_A(\alpha) = \frac{(4-\alpha)^2\left[1+(1-\alpha)^2\right] + (4-3\alpha+\alpha^2)^2 + (4-3\alpha)^2}{32}.
\end{equation}
As $\alpha$ rises $\kappa_B$ decreases monotonically to $9/8$ at $\alpha = 1$; it stays above 1 because the social male still contributes to his nest's productivity. $\kappa_A$ decreases to $7/16$, because males then gain nothing from their own breeding value.

Under strict monogamy ($\alpha = 0$) both models give $1 + \Psi = 2/[2 - Z(1 + r_{mf})] = Q$, so $L_f = Q(a_f + a_{m'})$ and Equation~\eqref{eq:kappa_def} becomes $1/(1 + Q^2C^2)$ with $C^2 = 2V_A(1 + r_{mf})$. This is the pair-mated model of Santiago and Caballero~\cite{santiago1995}, and $\kappa = 2$ at $Z = 1$, $r_{mf} = 0$. Under random mating, $u = a$ in both sexes, so $\bar{c} = 1$. Each offspring's other parent has expected breeding value $r_{mf}a$, so $h_1 = \theta a$ with $\theta = (Z/2)(1 + r_{mf})$. Then $L = a/(1-\theta) = Q\,a$ in both sexes and $\kappa = Q^2/4$, which is 1 at $Z = 1$, $r_{mf} = 0$. Positive assortment lengthens the persistence of an association through $\theta$ and so raises $\kappa$; we set $r_{mf} = 0$ for want of estimates.

Under random mating, and under Model B, a parent's own breeding value contributes to its family size with weight 1 in both sexes. Equation~\eqref{eq:Zcgf} therefore gives the same retention factor in both sexes: $Z = 1$ for a Gaussian breeding value and $Z_k = 1 - s_k$ for each class of deleterious alleles (Equation~\eqref{eq:Zclass}).

\subsection{Own, partner and cis components}\label{app:B_split}
The linked term of the main text needs $\kappa$ taken apart, because only part of the variance it counts can lie on the haplotype of a focal gene copy. This is where the individual's long-term contribution and the gene copy's background differ (\autoref{sec:unlinked}). With $r_{mf} = 0$, $\mathrm{Var}(L_f) + \mathrm{Var}(L_m)$ separates into terms in the carrier's own breeding value and in its social partner's:
\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
    \kappa = \kappa_\text{own} + \kappa_\text{partner}, \\
    \kappa_\text{own} = \frac{(1+\Psi)^2 + (m+\Psi)^2}{8}, \qquad
    \kappa_\text{partner} = \frac{\left[1+\Psi(1-\alpha)\right]^2 + (1-\alpha)^2(1+\Psi)^2}{8}.
\end{gathered}
\label{eq:kappa_split}
\end{equation}
The partner's genes are never on the focal copy's haplotype, so the partner component is wholly unlinked. Of the own component, the share on the focal haplotype ($v_h$) moves to the linked term. There a selected locus keeps its association with the copy with probability $\rho = (1-r)Z$ per generation, and the cumulative advantage per unit of the locus's effect is the linked kernel $1/(1-\rho) = 1 + \chi$ of \autoref{sec:linkage}, with $\chi = \rho/(1-\rho)$, when every carrier has weight 1. With the carrier's own weight $m_X$ (1 in females, $m$ in males) and later carriers' average weight $\bar{m}$, it is $m_X + \bar{m}\chi$ instead. The ratio of the squares, averaged over the sexes, is
\begin{equation}
    \kappa_\text{cis}(\rho) = \frac{\left[1 + \bar{m}\chi\right]^2 + \left[m + \bar{m}\chi\right]^2}{2(1+\chi)^2},
\end{equation}
which decreases from $(1+m^2)/2$ as $\rho \to 0$ to $\bar{m}^2$ under tight linkage. Under random mating, and under Model B, $m = 1$, so $\kappa_\text{own} = \kappa_\text{cis} = 1$ and $\kappa_\text{partner} = \kappa - 1$. Equation~\eqref{total_red} uses this split: $\kappa_\text{cis}$ on the linked term, and $Q^2(\kappa V_A - \kappa_\text{own}v_h)$ in the exponent of the unlinked background, with $Q = 2/(2-Z)$ at the same retention factor as the linked term.

\subsection{The multiplicative model with pair bonds}\label{app:B_mult}
Under Model B with $w \propto e^{z}$, a copy's long-term contribution mixes two routes. A copy in a female is in a nest of productivity $e^{z_f + z_{m'}}$, and a fraction $1 - \alpha$ of her offspring are sired by $m'$ and a fraction $\alpha$ by extra-pair males. A copy in a male gains within-pair offspring from his own nest and extra-pair offspring elsewhere, in proportion to $e^{z_m}$. From the next generation on, a carrier contributes in proportion to $e^{2a}$, where $a = z - \bar{z}$ is its deviation (Equation~\eqref{eq:A_R}), so
\begin{equation}
    R_f \propto (1-\alpha)\,e^{2a_f + 2a_{m'}} + \alpha c_f\,e^{2a_f + a_{m'}}, \qquad
    R_m \propto (1-\alpha)\,e^{2a_m + 2a_{f'}} + \alpha c_m\,e^{2a_m}.
\end{equation}
The constants $c_f$ and $c_m$ collect expectations over individuals outside the pair: the extra-pair sire, and the nests in which a male sires extra-pair young. They equal one at $V_A = 0$. In each sex, write $\mathbf{a} = (a_X, a_{X'})$ for the deviations of the carrier and its social partner, independent with variance $V_A$. Then $R = \sum_k c_k e^{\boldsymbol{\lambda}_k\cdot\mathbf{a}}$, with exponent vectors $\boldsymbol{\lambda} = (2,2), (2,1)$ in females and $(2,2), (2,0)$ in males. The coefficients are $c_1 = 1 - \alpha$ for the within-pair route, and $c_2 = \alpha c_f$ or $\alpha c_m$ for the extra-pair route. Gaussian moments, $\mathbb{E}[e^{\boldsymbol{\lambda}\cdot\mathbf{a}}] = e^{V_A|\boldsymbol{\lambda}|^2/2}$, give
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\mathbb{E}[R^2]}{\mathbb{E}[R]^2} = \sum_{k,l} w_k w_l\, e^{V_A\,\boldsymbol{\lambda}_k\cdot\boldsymbol{\lambda}_l}, \qquad
    w_k = \frac{c_k\, e^{V_A|\boldsymbol{\lambda}_k|^2/2}}{\sum_l c_l\, e^{V_A|\boldsymbol{\lambda}_l|^2/2}}, \label{eq:B_moments}
\end{equation}
where $w_k$ is the share of the copy's expected long-term contribution that passes through route $k$. $N/N_e$ is the mean of Equation~\eqref{eq:B_moments} over the sexes (Equation~\eqref{eq:A_ratio}). Every product $\boldsymbol{\lambda}_k\cdot\boldsymbol{\lambda}_l$ is at most 8, so the ratio is at most $e^{8V_A}$ in either sex, whatever the $c_k$. In the other direction, Jensen's inequality bounds the ratio below by $\exp(V_A|\bar{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}_X|^2)$, with $\bar{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}_X = \sum_k w_k\boldsymbol{\lambda}_k$, and the mean of two exponentials is at least the exponential of their mean. At $w = (1-\alpha, \alpha)$, $\tfrac12(|\bar{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}_f|^2 + |\bar{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}_m|^2) = 8 - 6\alpha + \tfrac52\alpha^2 = 4\kappa_B$, and $|\bar{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}_X|^2$ increases with the within-pair share. So whenever the extra-pair route carries no more than its share $\alpha$ of a copy's expected contribution,
\begin{equation}
    e^{-8V_A} \le \frac{N_e}{N} \le e^{-4\kappa_B V_A}. \label{eq:B_mult_pair}
\end{equation}
The extra-pair share is exactly $\alpha$ when extra-pair siring success is proportional to $e^{z}$, which gives $c_f = e^{3V_A/2}$ and $c_m = e^{2V_A}$. It is smaller when the extra-pair sire's breeding value is held at the mean, $c_f = c_m = 1$, the case computed in the main text. Numerically, the bracket also holds for $c_f$ and $c_m$ up to $e^{2V_A}$. Both ends are equalities at $\alpha = 0$, where $\kappa_B = 2$ and Equation~\eqref{eq:B_mult_pair} closes on Weissman and Barton's~\cite{weissman2012} $e^{-8V_A}$ (Equation~\eqref{eq:A_mono}). The right-hand end agrees with $1/(1 + 4\kappa_B V_A)$ for small $V_A$. As $V_A$ grows the exact value moves from the right-hand end toward the left, because the within-pair route, with the largest exponent, comes to dominate the second moment.
