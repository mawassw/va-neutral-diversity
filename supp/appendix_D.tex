\section{Mutation--selection--drift balance and the implied selection coefficient}\label{app:msdbapp}

\subsection{Stationary variance}\label{app:D_stationary}
Suppose deleterious mutations arise at rate $U_d$ per diploid genome per generation across $L_\text{target}$ independent sites in a population of constant effective size $N_e$. We define $U_d = 2\mu L_\text{target}$, with per-site mutation rate $\mu$ satisfying $4N_e\mu \ll 1$.  Mutations are semidominant with heterozygous effect $s \ll 1$ on relative fitness (genotype fitnesses $1$, $1-s$, $1-2s$), so a site at mutant frequency $q$ contributes $2q(1-q)s^2$ to the additive variance in relative fitness $V_A$. Under a distribution of fitness effects (DFE) with density $\varphi(s)$ and arithmetic mean $\bar{s}$, class $k$ has heterozygous effect $s_k$ and receives fraction $\varphi_k$ of new mutations.

For a semidominant deleterious allele, the stationary expected heterozygosity per site ($2q(1-q)$) under the diffusion approximation~\cite{kimura1969} is
\begin{equation}
    \mathbb{E}[2q(1-q)] = 8N_e\mu\left(\frac{1}{4N_e s} - \frac{1}{e^{4N_e s} - 1}\right).
\end{equation}
Multiplying by $s^2$ and summing over $L_\text{target}$ sites gives Equation~\eqref{eq:VA_MSD} of the main text:
\begin{equation}
    V_A = U_d\,s\left(1 - \frac{4N_e s}{e^{4N_e s} - 1}\right). \label{eq:D_VA_MSD}
\end{equation}
The parenthetical factor lies strictly in the range $[0, 1]$. For $N_e s \ll 1$, it expands to $2N_e s$, recovering the neutral mutation--drift variance $V_A \approx 2N_e U_d s^2$~\cite{lynch1986}. For $N_e s \gg 1$, it approaches $1$, as each allele is held near $q = \mu/s$ and $V_A \approx U_d s$ at mutation-selection balance~\cite{mukai1972,charlesworth2012}.

\subsection{Inverting for \texorpdfstring{$s^*$}{s*}}\label{app:D_inversion}
Because Equation~\eqref{eq:D_VA_MSD} is strictly increasing in $s$ from $0$ to $\infty$, any observed $V_A > 0$ determines a unique single-effect solution $s$. In the strong-selection limit, Equation~\eqref{eq:strong} inverts to $s^* = \bar{V}_A / U_d$ (Equation~\eqref{eq:sstar}), which is self-consistent whenever $2N_e s^* \gg 1$. \claude{A solution in the weak-selection regime ($2N_e s \ll 1$), where $V_A \approx 2N_e U_d s^2$, would require}
\begin{equation}
    N_e \ll \frac{U_d}{2V_A},
\end{equation}
corresponding to an effective size of only a few individuals for $U_d \sim O(1)$ and $V_A \sim O(0.1)$. Both $2N_e s^*$ and $U_d/(2V_A)$ are reported for each study population in the Results.

\subsection{Distributions of fitness effects}\label{app:D_dfe}
Summing Equation~\eqref{eq:D_VA_MSD} over selective classes gives the stationary variance maintained under a DFE:
\begin{equation}
    V_A^{\text{DFE}} = U_d \sum_k \varphi_k\, s_k\left(1 - \frac{4N_e s_k}{e^{4N_e s_k} - 1}\right) \le U_d\,\bar{s}.
\label{eq:VA_forward}
\end{equation}
Equality is attained in the strong-selection limit, where each class contributes $U_d\,\varphi_k s_k$ regardless of the shape of $\varphi(s)$\claude{, because a class of larger effect adds more variance per generation and is removed proportionally sooner (}\autoref{sec:msdb}\claude{)}; consequently, $s^*$ estimates the arithmetic mean $\bar{s}$. The shape of the DFE matters only through nearly neutral classes ($4N_e s_k \lesssim 1$), which contribute less than $U_d\,\varphi_k s_k$ and therefore lower $V_A^{\text{DFE}}$ for a fixed mean $\bar{s}$. Using the upper bound $U_d\,\bar{s}$ with the DFE assigned to each population~\cite{lin2026} (Methods) is thus \claude{biased toward passing the maintenance test, because} it overstates the variance maintainable by mutation--selection--drift balance. The class-specific retention factor $Z_k = 1 - s_k$ and variance share $\nu_k = \varphi_k s_k / \bar{s}$ are derived in Appendix~\ref{app:A_Z}.

\subsection{Unlinked reduction under fast purging}\label{app:D_purge}
Strongly deleterious alleles are purged within a few generations, causing their statistical associations with unlinked neutral sites to decay faster than through Mendelian segregation alone. At retention factor $Z_k = 1 - s_k$, the unlinked transmission factor (Equation~\eqref{eq:Q}) becomes $Q_k = 2/(1 + s_k)$ in place of $2$~\cite{santiago1998,charlesworth2012}. Weighting by each class's variance share $\nu_k = \varphi_k s_k / \bar{s}$ of $V_A = U_d\bar{s}$ yields
\begin{equation}
    \sum_k \nu_k\,Q_k^2\,V_A = 4U_d\,\bar{s}_\text{eff}, \qquad \bar{s}_\text{eff} = \sum_k \varphi_k\,\frac{s_k}{(1+s_k)^2} < \bar{s}.
\label{eq:D_purge}
\end{equation}
\claude{Large effects are discounted because their carriers are removed before the association has lasted its two generations.} Equation~\eqref{eq:D_purge} replaces $4\kappa V_A$ by $4\kappa U_d\bar{s}_\text{eff}$ in the reduction at unlinked sites, in the multiplicative model or its small-variance approximation. The substitution is exact under random mating and under strict monogamy. With extra-pair paternity, $\kappa$ itself depends on $Z$ (Equation~\eqref{eq:B_kappa_general}), and holding it at its value for $Z = 1$ understates the reduction in $N_e$, by at most 2.3\% for $s_k \le 0.1$. Like truncation of the upper tail of log-fitness (Appendix~\ref{app:A_tail}), purging lowers the reduction in $N_e$ below the multiplicative bound used in the main text. Results give the size of this purging correction at $s^*$.

\subsection{Equivalence to the unlinked limit of background selection}\label{app:D_bgs}
Equation~\eqref{eq:D_purge} coincides with the unlinked limit of classical background selection. Consider a diploid selected locus with haploid (per-gamete) deleterious mutation rate $\mu_\text{locus}$ and heterozygous effect $s$. At mutation--selection balance ($q \approx \mu_\text{locus}/s$), the locus contributes $2qs^2 = 2\mu_\text{locus}s$ to $V_A$, or $\mu_\text{locus}s$ per homolog. For a neutral site at recombination fraction $r$, the background-selection reduction from the single \textit{cis} copy is $\exp\left[-\mu_\text{locus}s / (s + r(1-s))^2\right]$~\cite{hudson1995,nordborg1996}, which at $r = \tfrac12$ reduces to $\exp\left\{-[2/(1+s)]^2\mu_\text{locus}s\right\}$, recovering the transmission factor $Q_k = 2/(1+s_k)$ of Equation~\eqref{eq:D_purge}.

At an unlinked locus, however, the \textit{trans} (homologous) copy reshuffles at the same rate $r = \tfrac12$ as the \textit{cis} copy and makes an identical contribution, so the diploid locus lowers $N_e/N$ by $\exp\left\{-[2/(1+s)]^2\,2\mu_\text{locus}s\right\}$. Summing over all loci with $U_d = \sum 2\mu_\text{locus}$ yields the exponent $4U_d\bar{s}_\text{eff}$ of Equation~\eqref{eq:D_purge} under random mating; summing over \textit{cis} copies alone captures only half of the unlinked reduction. On the same chromosome, this identical accounting motivates Equation~\eqref{total_red}, where the \textit{cis} half of the variance ($v_h = v_i/2$) enters the local linked term while the homologous half remains in the unlinked background (whereas Buffalo and Kern~\cite{buffaloKern2024} track only the \textit{cis} half). Similarly, Marsh, Daigle, and Johri~\cite[Equation~4]{marsh2026} evaluate the unlinked background-selection contribution from other chromosomes across uniform DFE bins by averaging $\ln(1 + s) + (1 + s)^{-1}$ over each bin. Because $\frac{d}{ds}\left[\ln(1 + s) + (1 + s)^{-1}\right] = s/(1 + s)^2$, their unlinked exponent equals $4U_d\bar{s}_\text{eff}$ (both homologs included), and their simulation finding that weak-purging expressions ($s \ll 1$) overestimate unlinked diversity loss under strong selection reflects precisely the fast-purging factor $Q_k = 2/(1 + s_k)$ derived above.
