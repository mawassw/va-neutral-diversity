\section{Stationary Equilibrium and Implied Selection Coefficients}\label{app:msdbapp}

This appendix states the variance in fitness that recurrent deleterious mutation maintains at mutation--selection--drift balance, and inverts it to the selection coefficient a measured $V_A$ would require. Every result in it is established; we collect them in the notation of the main text and cite their sources. What is ours is the application to wild-vertebrate $V_A$ in total fitness. The comparison itself has a direct precedent: Charlesworth (2015) set observed additive variance in \emph{Drosophila} fitness components against the mutation--selection prediction and concluded that there is too much of it to be explained by mutation--selection balance (see also Johnson and Barton 2005).

\subsection*{D.1\quad Stationary variance}

Let each of $U_d$ new deleterious mutations per diploid genome per generation have heterozygous effect $s$ on relative fitness (fitnesses $1$, $1-s$, $1-2s$), so its average effect is $s$ and a site at frequency $q$ contributes $2q(1-q)s^2$ to $V_A$. A new mutation enters at $q = 1/(2N)$, so the influx of variance is $V_m = U_d s^2$ (Charlesworth 2015, Equation 1). Selection removes a rare allele's variance at rate $s$ and drift removes heterozygosity at rate $1/(2N_e)$, so a flux balance gives
\begin{equation}
    V_A \approx \frac{V_m}{s + 1/(2N_e)} = \frac{2N_eU_ds^2}{1+2N_es}.
\label{eq:D_VA_interp}
\end{equation}
This interpolates between the mutation--drift limit $V_A = 2N_eV_m$ for $N_es \ll 1$ (Lynch and Hill 1986) and the deterministic rare-allele balance $V_A = U_ds$, $q = \mu/s$, for $N_es \gg 1$ (Mukai et al. 1972; Charlesworth 2012). It has the same form as the stochastic house-of-cards variance of Bürger et al. (1989).

Equation~\eqref{eq:D_VA_interp} is a heuristic, not the diffusion result. Drift does not change the expected allele frequency, and under irreversible mutation $\mathbb{E}[q]$ has no stationary value, so the balance has to be written for expected heterozygosity. The diffusion solution for a semidominant deleterious allele (Kimura 1969) gives, per site, $\mathbb{E}[H] = 8N_e\mu\left[1/\beta - 1/(e^\beta - 1)\right]$ with $\beta = 4N_es$, and therefore
\begin{equation}
    V_A = U_d\,s\left[1 - \frac{4N_es}{e^{4N_es}-1}\right].
\label{eq:D_VA_exact}
\end{equation}
% UNVERIFIED: equation number in Kimura (1969) not checked; formula confirmed by quadrature of the sojourn density and a Wright-Fisher simulation (2Ns = 2).
Both expressions share their two limits. Between them Equation~\eqref{eq:D_VA_interp} is low by up to 29\%, at $4N_es \approx 3$. Our data lie deep in the strong-selection limit ($2N_es^* = 9{,}400$ to $390{,}000$ where $N_e = \pi/4\mu$ is available), where the two agree to better than one part in $10^3$.

\subsection*{D.2\quad The implied selection coefficient}

In the strong-selection limit, Equation~\eqref{eq:D_VA_exact} inverts to
\begin{equation}
    s^* = \frac{V_A}{U_d}.
\label{eq:sHoC}
\end{equation}
With $U_d = 4\mu L_\text{coding}$ (Methods) and the direct mutation-rate estimates, $U_d = 0.34$--$1.26$ across the 18 populations with an annotated genome. The posterior modes of $V_A$ then give $s^* = 0.006$--$0.78$, median 0.16, and $P(s^* > 0.01) > 0.89$ in 17 of the 18 posteriors (0.71 in the snow vole). The limit is self-consistent: $2N_es^*$ is $9{,}400$ or more in every population where $N_e$ can be computed. The weak-selection branch fails its own premise. $2N_es \ll 1$ with $V_A \approx 2N_eU_ds^2$ requires $N_e \ll U_d/(2V_A)$, about 3 for the median population (range 0.6--81).

Against a mean heterozygous effect of order $10^{-3}$ to $10^{-2}$ in vertebrate DFEs (Eyre-Walker and Keightley 2007; Huber et al. 2017), $s^*$ is one to two orders of magnitude too large. Read forward, $V_A = U_ds$ at $s = 10^{-3}$, $5 \times 10^{-3}$ and $10^{-2}$ falls short of the measured median $V_A$ by factors of 160, 32 and 16.

\paragraph{Retention of associations.} An association between a neutral site and a deleterious allele of effect $s$ survives a generation with probability $(1-s)(1-r)$ (Nordborg et al. 1996; Charlesworth 2012, Appendix), so the retention of the main text is $Z = 1 - s$ in the strong-selection limit ($1 - s - 1/(2N_e)$ with drift), whatever the size of $V_A$. Under a distribution of effects each class $k$ keeps its own $Z_k = 1 - s_k$ and carries the share $\nu_k = \varphi_ks_k/\bar{s}$ of $V_A$, and interference is averaged over classes with these weights (Theory). The total variance decays at the aggregate rate $V_m/V_A = \mathbb{E}[s^2]/\mathbb{E}[s] = \bar{s}(1 + \mathrm{CV}_s^2)$, the $\nu$-weighted mean of the $s_k$ (Santiago and Caballero 1998). It is well above $\bar{s}$ for leptokurtic DFEs, $6\bar{s}$ for a gamma DFE of shape 0.2, but because interference is nonlinear in $Z$ it is not used in place of the classes.

\subsection*{D.3\quad Distributions of fitness effects}

Let heterozygous effects $s$ follow a density $\varphi(s)$. In the strong-selection limit each class contributes $U_d\,s$, so
\begin{equation}
    V_A = U_d \int_0^\infty \varphi(s)\,s\,ds = U_d\,\bar{s}, \qquad s^* = \frac{V_A}{U_d},
\label{eq:VA_DFE}
\end{equation}
whatever the shape of the DFE. The inversion of Equation~\eqref{eq:sHoC} therefore applies to its arithmetic mean. Shape enters only through nearly neutral mutations ($4N_es \lesssim 1$), which contribute less than $U_ds$ (Equation~\eqref{eq:D_VA_exact} applied class by class). A more leptokurtic DFE with the same mean puts more mass there, which lowers the sustainable $V_A$ and widens the gap. Two cautions apply. DFEs inferred from site-frequency spectra do not constrain the strongly deleterious tail, so their mean is partly an extrapolation. And the variance-weighted mean $\mathbb{E}[s^2]/\mathbb{E}[s]$, not $\bar{s}$, is the aggregate rate $V_m/V_A$; the retention itself is set class by class.

\paragraph{Forward prediction.} Summing Equation~\eqref{eq:D_VA_exact} over a discretised DFE gives the variance a given DFE can sustain:
\begin{equation}
    V_A^{\text{DFE}} = U_d \sum_k \varphi_k\, s_k\left[1 - \frac{4N_es_k}{e^{4N_es_k}-1}\right],
\label{eq:VA_forward}
\end{equation}
with $\varphi_k$ the fraction of new mutations in class $k$ and $s_k$ its mean effect. With vertebrate-calibrated classes (Rettelbach et al. 2025) this yields $V_A^{\text{DFE}} \approx$ [XX].

\paragraph{Unlinked interference under fast purging.} Deleterious alleles of large effect are purged within a few generations, so their associations with unlinked neutral sites decay faster than by Mendelian segregation alone: at retention $Z_k = 1 - s_k$ the unlinked factor of the main text (Equation~\eqref{eq:Q}) is $Q = 2/(1+s_k)$ in place of 2. Following Charlesworth (2012, Appendix) and Santiago and Caballero (1998), the unlinked term becomes
\begin{equation}
    \frac{N_e}{N} = \frac{1}{1 + 4\kappa\,U_d\,\bar{s}_\text{eff}}, \qquad \bar{s}_\text{eff} = \int_0^\infty \varphi(s)\,\frac{s}{(1+s)^2}\,ds < \bar{s},
\label{eq:B_corrected}
\end{equation}
which replaces $4\kappa V_A = 4\kappa U_d\bar{s}$. This is the class-by-class average of $Q^2$ with the weights $\nu_k$. It acts in the same direction as truncation of the upper tail of log-fitness (Appendix~\ref{app:A_tail}): both lower the reduction below the multiplicative upper bound. At the implied $s^*$, the factor $1/(1+s^*)^2$ is 0.31--0.99 (median 0.74). The diversity predictions of the main text, which take $Q = 2$, therefore overstate the unlinked reduction at the selection coefficients the measured $V_A$ would require.

\paragraph{The unlinked limit of background selection.} Equation~\eqref{eq:B_corrected} is the global effective size of the main text written for deleterious mutations, and it is also what background selection becomes at unlinked loci. Consider a selected locus with deleterious mutation rate $u$ per gamete and heterozygous effect $s$. At equilibrium the mutant frequency is $q \approx u/s$, each of an individual's two copies carries variance $qs^2 = us$, and the locus contributes $2us$ to $V_A$. The single-locus background-selection effect on a neutral site at recombination fraction $r$ (Nordborg et al. 1996) is $\exp[-us/(s + r(1-s))^2]$; at $r = 1/2$ it is $\exp[-Q(s)^2\,us]$ with $Q(s) = 2/(1+s)$, the cumulative factor times the variance of the copy in cis. At an unlinked locus the homologous copy is reshuffled exactly as often as the cis copy and contributes the same amount, so the locus reduces $N_e/N$ by $Q(s)^2 \cdot 2us$, and the sum over loci, $4U_d\,\bar{s}_\text{eff}$ with $U_d = \sum 2u$, is Equation~\eqref{eq:B_corrected} to first order. A sum of the single-locus effect over cis copies alone therefore recovers half of the global reduction. Within a chromosome the same accounting gives the partition of Equation~\eqref{total_red}: the cis variance $v_h = v_i/2$ enters the local term, and the homologous half stays in the global one.
