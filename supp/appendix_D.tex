\section{The linked factor along a Haldane map}\label{app:chromosapp}
This appendix derives the chromosome-wide average $\overline{Q^2}_i$ of \autoref{sec:linkage} (Equation~\eqref{eq:haldane_avg}) and evaluates it.

\subsection{The kernel and chromosome average}\label{app:D_kernel}
Consider a single mutational effect class with retention factor $Z$ (Equation~\eqref{eq:Z}); under a distribution of fitness effects, each expression below is averaged over classes using the variance weights $\nu_k$ of \autoref{sec:linkage}. Take a chromosome of map length $M$ Morgans (dropping the chromosome index $i$ for clarity) on which two sites separated by $\ell$ Morgans recombine with probability $r(\ell)$. Unless stated otherwise (Section~\ref{app:D_spatial}), both selected variance and neutral sites are distributed uniformly in map distance. Let $Q^2 = [2/(2-Z)]^2$ denote the unlinked transmission factor squared (Equation~\eqref{eq:Q}), $\overline{Q^2}(y)$ the linked value at a neutral site at position $y$ averaged over selected loci, and $\overline{Q^2}_\text{H}$ and $\overline{Q^2}_\text{L}$ the chromosome-wide averages under the Haldane and linear maps ($\overline{Q^2}_\text{H}$ is the $\overline{Q^2}_i$ of the main text).

An association between a neutral site and a selected locus $\ell$ Morgans away survives one generation with probability $(1 - r(\ell))Z$, as recombination breaks it with probability $r(\ell)$ and selection multiplies the remaining association by $Z$. Summing over generations gives the cumulative advantage per unit effect~\cite{santiago2016,buffaloKern2024},
\begin{equation}
    \sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\left[(1 - r(\ell))\,Z\right]^t = \frac{1}{1-(1-r(\ell))Z}.
\end{equation}
It is the counterpart of $Q$ for the chromosome segment that carries the neutral site, counting the advantage over every generation in which the segment keeps the selected locus, so it is the expected number of generations that the association lasts (\autoref{sec:linkage}). Because the cumulative factor appears squared in the variance of the segment's long-term contributions, as $Q$ does in Equation~\eqref{eq:B_first}, a locus at distance $\ell$ contributes $\mathcal{K}(\ell) = [1 - (1 - r(\ell))Z]^{-2}$. Here and below, $y$ is always the position of a neutral site and $x$ that of a selected locus. With $\mathcal{I}(\ell) = \int_0^\ell \mathcal{K}(\ell')\,d\ell'$, the profile and its chromosome average are
\begin{gather}
    \overline{Q^2}(y) = \frac{1}{M}\int_0^M \mathcal{K}(\vert x-y\vert)\,dx = \frac{1}{M}\left[\mathcal{I}(y) + \mathcal{I}(M-y)\right], \label{eq:D_profile} \\
    \frac{1}{M}\int_0^M \overline{Q^2}(y)\,dy = \frac{2}{M^2}\int_0^M (M-\ell)\,\mathcal{K}(\ell)\,d\ell = \frac{2}{M^2}\int_0^M \mathcal{I}(\ell)\,d\ell, \label{eq:D_haldane_avg}
\end{gather}
where the factor $(M - \ell)$ weights each distance $\ell$ by the density of site pairs separated by $\ell$. Under Haldane's mapping function, $r_\text{H}(\ell) = \tfrac12(1 - e^{-2\ell})$, which saturates at $\tfrac12$ for sites far apart on the same chromosome, Equation~\eqref{eq:D_haldane_avg} becomes, for chromosome $i$ of map length $M_i$,
\begin{equation}
    \overline{Q^2}_i = \frac{2}{M_i^2}\int_0^{M_i} (M_i - \ell)\left(\frac{1}{1 - \dfrac{Z}{2}(1 + e^{-2\ell})}\right)^2 d\ell. \label{eq:haldane_avg}
\end{equation}
Substituting $Z = 1 - s$ reduces the term inside the integral to $1/[s + r(\ell)(1-s)]$, the familiar kernel from classical background selection theory~\cite{hudson1995,nordborg1996,santiago1998,santiago2016,buffaloKern2024}. Whereas Buffalo and Kern~\cite{buffaloKern2024} evaluate this kernel locally along a high-resolution recombination map, our spatial average yields the expected chromosome-wide reduction when only total map length $M_i$ is known. Santiago and Caballero~\cite{santiago1998} and Buffalo and Kern~\cite[Supplementary Equations~32--33]{buffaloKern2024} derived similar chromosome-wide averages for a central or end site under a linear map. Equation~\eqref{eq:haldane_avg} uses Haldane's mapping function and also averages over the position of the neutral site.

\subsection{Closed form under the Haldane map}\label{app:D_closed}
Under Haldane's map, $(1 - r_\text{H}(\ell))Z = \tfrac{Z}{2}(1 + e^{-2\ell})$, so
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{K}_\text{H}(\ell) = \left(\Gamma - \eta\, e^{-2\ell}\right)^{-2}, \qquad \Gamma = 1 - \tfrac{Z}{2}, \quad \eta = \tfrac{Z}{2}.
\end{equation}
Substituting $\tau = e^{-2\ell}$ and decomposing into partial fractions gives the arm integral in elementary functions:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{I}_\text{H}(\ell) = \frac{1}{2}\left\{\frac{2\ell + \ln\!\left[(\Gamma - \eta e^{-2\ell})/(1-Z)\right]}{\Gamma^2} + \frac{1}{\Gamma}\left[\frac{1}{1-Z} - \frac{1}{\Gamma - \eta e^{-2\ell}}\right]\right\}.
\label{eq:D_arm}
\end{equation}
Integrating once more over $[0, M]$, using $\int_0^M \ln(\Gamma - \eta e^{-2\ell})\,d\ell = M\ln \Gamma + \tfrac{1}{2}\left[\mathrm{Li}_2(\epsilon e^{-2M}) - \mathrm{Li}_2(\epsilon)\right]$ with $\epsilon = \eta/\Gamma$ and $\mathrm{Li}_2$ the dilogarithm, gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
    \overline{Q^2}_\text{H} = \frac{1}{M^2}\Bigg\{ &\frac{M^2 - M\ln(1-Z) + M\ln \Gamma + \tfrac{1}{2}\left[\mathrm{Li}_2(\epsilon e^{-2M}) - \mathrm{Li}_2(\epsilon)\right]}{\Gamma^2} \\
    &+ \frac{1}{\Gamma}\left[\frac{M}{1-Z} - \frac{2M + \ln\!\left[(\Gamma-\eta e^{-2M})/(1-Z)\right]}{2\Gamma}\right]\Bigg\}.
\end{split}
\label{eq:D_closed_haldane}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:D_closed_haldane} is the exact form evaluated in the main analysis. It agrees with direct numerical quadrature of Equation~\eqref{eq:D_haldane_avg} in R to better than $10^{-11}$ relative error across $Z \in [10^{-9}, 1 - 10^{-9}]$ and $M \in [0.005, 60]$ Morgans, spanning all selection classes and map lengths analyzed.

\subsection{Short- and long-map limits}\label{app:D_limits}
As $M \to 0$, $\overline{Q^2}_\text{H} \to (1-Z)^{-2}$, the non-recombining limit of background selection. Approaching this limit requires recombination across the entire chromosome to be slower than purging ($M \ll 1 - Z$), not only a short physical map. At $Z = 0.99$ and $M = 0.5$, $\overline{Q^2}_\text{H} = 387$ compared with $(1-Z)^{-2} = 10^4$. At the other extreme, as $\ell \to \infty$, $\mathcal{K}_\text{H}(\ell) \to \Gamma^{-2} = Q^2$, recovering the unlinked factor, and on a freely recombining chromosome ($M \to \infty$) the linked and unlinked terms of Equation~\eqref{total_red} combine into the reduction at unlinked sites, $e^{-Q^2\kappa V_A}$. Convergence to this free-recombination limit is slow:
\begin{equation}
    \overline{Q^2}_\text{H} = Q^2 + \frac{2}{M}\int_0^\infty \left[\mathcal{K}_\text{H}(\ell) - Q^2\right]d\ell + O(M^{-2}).
\end{equation}
From Equation~\eqref{eq:D_arm}, the leading integral evaluates to $\frac{1}{2\Gamma}\left[\frac{1}{1-Z} - \frac{1}{\Gamma} + \frac{\ln(\Gamma/(1-Z))}{\Gamma}\right] \sim (1-Z)^{-1}$ as $Z \to 1$ ($10.3$ at $Z = 0.9$ and $105$ at $Z = 0.99$). Free recombination therefore requires $M \gg 1/(1-Z)$; even at $M = 10$ and $Z = 0.99$, $\overline{Q^2}_\text{H} = 24.8$ against $Q^2 = 3.9$.

Empirical chromosomes occupy the intermediate regime $\overline{Q^2}_\text{H} \approx 2/[M(1-Z)]$~\cite{santiago1998}, because an allele removed at rate $s = 1 - Z$ stays linked to sites within about $s$ Morgans on either side, a fraction $2s/M$ of the chromosome, for about $1/s$ generations, which gives $(1/s)^2 \times 2s/M$. For the same reason the reduction depends on the selected target per Morgan, $f_i/M_i$. Even avian microchromosomes rarely approach the non-recombining limit, because an obligate crossover per bivalent implies $M \gtrsim 0.5$ Morgans, and the shortest empirical maps analyzed here ($0.04$--$0.2$ Morgans in great tits and song sparrows) still greatly exceed $1 - Z$ for all but the classes purged fastest (Table~\ref{tab:linked_limits}).

\subsection{The linear map as a lower bound}\label{app:D_linear}
Under a linear map, $r_\text{L}(\ell) = \ell$, the kernel is $\mathcal{K}_\text{L}(\ell) = (1 - Z + \ell Z)^{-2}$ and the arm integral is $\mathcal{I}_\text{L}(\ell) = \ell/[(1-Z)(1-Z+\ell Z)]$, whose mid-chromosome value matches Supplementary Equation~33 of Buffalo and Kern~\cite{buffaloKern2024}. Equation~\eqref{eq:D_haldane_avg} gives
\begin{equation}
    \overline{Q^2}_\text{L} = \frac{2}{M^2(1-Z)}\int_0^M \frac{y}{1-Z+yZ}\,dy = \frac{2}{MZ(1-Z)} - \frac{2}{M^2Z^2}\ln\!\left(1+\frac{MZ}{1-Z}\right).
\label{eq:closed_form}
\end{equation}
As $M \to 0$, $\overline{Q^2}_\text{L} = (1-Z)^{-2} - \tfrac{2}{3}MZ(1-Z)^{-3} + O(M^2)$, matching $\overline{Q^2}_\text{H}$ to first order because $r_\text{H}(\ell) = \ell + O(\ell^2)$. As $M \to \infty$, however, $\overline{Q^2}_\text{L} \to 0$ rather than $Q^2$, because $r_\text{L}(\ell)$ exceeds $1/2$ for $\ell > 1/2$.

Because $e^{-2\ell} > 1 - 2\ell$ implies $r_\text{H}(\ell) < \ell$ for all $\ell > 0$, it follows that $0 < 1 - Z + r_\text{H}(\ell)Z < 1 - Z + \ell Z$ everywhere for $Z \in (0, 1)$, so $\mathcal{K}_\text{L}(\ell) < \mathcal{K}_\text{H}(\ell)$ at every distance and
\begin{equation}
    \overline{Q^2}_\text{L} < \overline{Q^2}_\text{H} \qquad \text{for all } M > 0.
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:closed_form} is therefore a lower bound on $\overline{Q^2}_i$, and hence on the reduction in $N_e$ at linked sites. The gap grows with $M$ and with $1-Z$; the ratio $\overline{Q^2}_\text{L}/\overline{Q^2}_\text{H}$ is 0.98, 0.95, and 0.91 at $M = 0.1$, 1, and 3 Morgans for $Z = 0.99$, and 0.99, 0.85, and 0.67 for $Z = 0.9$. Buffalo and Kern~\cite{buffaloKern2024} use instead the linear-map profile at the middle of the segment, $\overline{Q^2}_\text{L}(M/2) = 1/[(1-Z)(1-Z+ZM/2)]$, which is the largest value along the segment. The mid-segment value exceeds $\overline{Q^2}_\text{H}$ by at most about 12\%, on short maps with $M$ a few times $1-Z$, and is below it once $M$ exceeds about one Morgan and purging is fast, to 0.81 at $M = 10$ for $Z = 0.99$ and 0.41 for $Z = 0.9$ (Figure~\ref{fig:S_maps}). Both linear-map expressions are close to the Haldane average over the rest of the range, which includes the short, densely mapped segments of human data for which the mid-segment value was designed. The main text uses the Haldane closed form, Equation~\eqref{eq:D_closed_haldane}.

\subsection{Upper bound on the linked factor (\texorpdfstring{$\Omega_i \le 1$}{Omega <= 1})}\label{app:omega}
Linkage can only lengthen the association between a neutral site and the selected alleles on its haplotype, so their variance never counts for less than it would at an unlinked site. The argument below makes this exact. Because $\mathcal{K}_\text{H}(\ell)$ decreases monotonically from $(1-Z)^{-2}$ to $Q^2$, every local profile satisfies $\overline{Q^2}(y) \ge Q^2$, and therefore $\overline{Q^2}_i \ge Q^2$ regardless of how selected variance is distributed along the chromosome (Section~\ref{app:D_spatial}). We define three compound quantities
\begin{equation}
    \Lambda_\text{linked} = \kappa_\text{cis}\overline{Q^2}_iv_h, \qquad \Lambda_\text{own} = \kappa_\text{own}Q^2v_h, \qquad \Lambda_\text{unlinked} = Q^2\kappa V_A.
\end{equation}
In the primary model $\kappa_\text{cis} = \kappa_\text{own}$ (Appendix~\ref{app:C_split}), so $\Lambda_\text{linked} \ge \Lambda_\text{own}$; furthermore, $\Lambda_\text{unlinked} \ge \Lambda_\text{own}$ since $v_h \le V_A$ and $\kappa \ge \kappa_\text{own}$. Under the multiplicative form of Equation~\eqref{total_red}, $\Omega_i = e^{-(\Lambda_\text{linked} - \Lambda_\text{own})} \le 1$ follows immediately, with equality on a freely recombining chromosome ($\overline{Q^2}_i \to Q^2$), because there no association outlasts an unlinked one, so linkage adds nothing.

The bound also holds when the unlinked factor uses the small-variance reciprocal form $1/(1 + \Lambda_\text{unlinked} - \Lambda_\text{own})$, because $e^{-\Lambda_\text{linked}} \le e^{-\Lambda_\text{own}} \le 1/(1+\Lambda_\text{own})$,
\begin{equation}
    \Omega_i = e^{-\Lambda_\text{linked}}\,\frac{1+\Lambda_\text{unlinked}}{1+\Lambda_\text{unlinked}-\Lambda_\text{own}} \le \frac{1+\Lambda_\text{unlinked}}{(1+\Lambda_\text{own})(1+\Lambda_\text{unlinked}-\Lambda_\text{own})} \le 1,
\end{equation}
where the final inequality holds because $(1+\Lambda_\text{own})(1+\Lambda_\text{unlinked}-\Lambda_\text{own}) - (1+\Lambda_\text{unlinked}) = \Lambda_\text{own}(\Lambda_\text{unlinked}-\Lambda_\text{own}) \ge 0$. Under free recombination, this mixed form gives $\Omega_i = 1 - \Lambda_\text{own}\Lambda_\text{unlinked}/(1 + \Lambda_\text{unlinked}) + O(\Lambda_\text{own}^2)$, an $O(v_h V_A)$ discrepancy arising solely from combining exponential and reciprocal approximations for the same variance. Under a distribution of fitness effects, the bound applies class by class.

With linkage, the minimum variance that accounts for $\delta$ solves Equation~\eqref{eq:genome_linked} set equal to $\delta$ (Methods), and because $\overline{\Omega} \le 1$ it lies below the unlinked value at the same retention factor, $\ln(1/\delta)/(Q^2\kappa)$, which equals $V_{A,\delta}$ at $Z = 1$ and $\kappa = \kappa_\text{max}$.

\subsection{Non-uniform placement of selected variance}\label{app:D_spatial}
Because the chromosomal architecture of fitness variance in wild populations is unknown~\cite{barton2002}, uniform placement is a baseline assumption. Let $g(x) \ge 0$ ($\int_0^M g(x)\,dx = 1$) denote the density of selected variance in map distance. With neutral sites uniform in map distance, symmetry of $\mathcal{K}_\text{H}(\vert x-y\vert)$ gives
\begin{equation}
    \overline{Q^2}_\text{H}[g] = \frac{1}{M}\int_0^M\!\!\int_0^M g(x)\, \mathcal{K}_\text{H}(\vert x-y\vert)\,dx\,dy = \int_0^M g(x)\,\overline{Q^2}(x)\,dx, \label{general_g}
\end{equation}
where the square brackets mark the chromosome average as a function of the whole density $g$. Because $\mathcal{K}_\text{H}$ depends only on the distance between the two sites, the profile of Equation~\eqref{eq:D_profile} has two equivalent meanings. Evaluated at a neutral site, it averages the kernel over selected loci spread uniformly; evaluated at a selected locus, as here, it averages the kernel over neutral sites spread uniformly, and so measures how much a locus at $x$ reduces diversity along the whole chromosome. With $g = 1/M$ the chromosome average equals the uniform one, $\overline{Q^2}_\text{H}[1/M] = \overline{Q^2}_\text{H}$, and for $J$ loci of equal effect at $x_1, \ldots, x_J$ it is the mean of $\overline{Q^2}(x_j)$. Because $\overline{Q^2}(x) \ge Q^2$ everywhere, $\Omega_i \le 1$ holds for every $g$. The profile is strictly concave, because its second derivative, $[\mathcal{K}_\text{H}'(x) + \mathcal{K}_\text{H}'(M-x)]/M$, is negative. It is largest at the midpoint and smallest at the telomeres, because a central locus is linked to neutral sites on both chromosome arms and a terminal one to sites on one arm only.

Because linked diversity depends on the expectation of the convex function $\exp(-\kappa_\text{cis} v_h \overline{Q^2})$, Jensen's inequality implies that for a single locus placed uniformly at random on $[0, M]$,
\begin{equation}
    \mathbb{E}_{x_0}\!\left[\exp\!\left(-\kappa_\text{cis} v_h \overline{Q^2}(x_0)\right)\right] > \exp\!\left(-\kappa_\text{cis} v_h \overline{Q^2}_\text{H}\right).
\end{equation}
For $J$ independently placed equal-effect loci, a second-order expansion gives
\begin{equation}
    \mathbb{E}\!\left[\exp\!\left(-\kappa_\text{cis} v_h \overline{Q^2}_\text{H}[g]\right)\right] - \exp\!\left(-\kappa_\text{cis} v_h \overline{Q^2}_\text{H}\right) \approx \frac{(\kappa_\text{cis} v_h)^2}{2J}\exp\!\left(-\kappa_\text{cis} v_h \overline{Q^2}_\text{H}\right)\mathrm{Var}_x\!\left[\overline{Q^2}(x)\right].
\end{equation}
Under random placement independent of recombination rate, uniform density therefore maximizes the expected linked reduction in $N_e$. This ordering can reverse, however, when selected alleles are concentrated in regions of suppressed recombination such as pericentromeres or chromosomal inversions. Even without physical clustering, a region of low recombination places long stretches of selected and neutral sequence within a short map interval, so linkage there is tighter than under uniform placement. Processes that concentrate selected alleles in low-recombination regions or inversions~\cite{kirkpatrick2006,yeaman2013} add to this effect.
