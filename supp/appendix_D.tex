\section{Mutation--selection--drift balance and the implied selection coefficient}\label{app:msdbapp}
This appendix derives the variance that recurrent deleterious mutation maintains at mutation--selection--drift balance (Equation~\eqref{eq:VA_MSD}). It also gives the conditions under which that variance inverts to the implied selection coefficient of Equation~\eqref{eq:sstar}, the extension to a distribution of fitness effects, and the reduction in effective size at unlinked sites when deleterious alleles are purged quickly. Deleterious mutations are assumed to arise at rate $U_d$ per diploid genome per generation, each at a new site. They are also assumed to be semidominant, with heterozygous effect $s$ on relative fitness (fitnesses $1$, $1-s$, $1-2s$), so a site at frequency $q$ contributes $2q(1-q)s^2$ to $V_A$. Under a distribution of fitness effects (DFE) with density $\varphi(s)$ and mean $\bar{s}$, class $k$ has effect $s_k$ and receives a fraction $\varphi_k$ of new mutations. The effective size $N_e$ is taken to be constant.

\subsection{Stationary variance}\label{app:D_stationary}
For a semidominant deleterious allele, Kimura's diffusion solution~\cite{kimura1969} gives the expected heterozygosity per site, $\mathbb{E}[H] = 8N_e\mu\left[1/(4N_es) - 1/(e^{4N_es} - 1)\right]$, with $\mu$ the mutation rate per site. Each site contributes $\mathbb{E}[H]\,s^2$ to $V_A$. Summing over the $L$ sites of the target, with $U_d = 2\mu L$, gives Equation~\eqref{eq:VA_MSD}, $V_A = U_d\,s\left[1 - 4N_es/(e^{4N_es} - 1)\right]$.
% UNVERIFIED: equation number in Kimura (1969) not checked; formula confirmed by quadrature of the sojourn density and a Wright-Fisher simulation (2Ns = 2).
The bracket in Equation~\eqref{eq:VA_MSD} never exceeds one. For $N_es \ll 1$ it is approximately $2N_es$, and $V_A$ is the mutation--drift variance $2N_eU_ds^2$~\cite{lynch1986}. For $N_es \gg 1$ it approaches one, because each allele is held near $q = \mu/s$ (mutation--selection balance), and $V_A = U_ds$~\cite{mukai1972,charlesworth2012}. The flux balance $V_A \approx 2N_eU_ds^2/(1 + 2N_es)$ has the same two limits~\cite[cf.][]{burger1989}, but it underestimates the variance by up to 29\% between them, at $4N_es \approx 3$, so we do not use it.

\subsection{Inverting for \texorpdfstring{$s^*$}{s*}, and why the weak-selection branch fails}\label{app:D_inversion}
In the strong-selection limit Equation~\eqref{eq:strong} inverts to Equation~\eqref{eq:sstar}, $s^* = \bar{V}_A/U_d$, which is self-consistent when $2N_es^* \gg 1$. The weak-selection branch cannot hold in its place. With $V_A \approx 2N_eU_ds^2$, the condition $2N_es \ll 1$ requires
\begin{equation}
    N_e \ll \frac{U_d}{2V_A},
\end{equation}
an effective size of a few individuals for $U_d$ of order one and $V_A$ of order 0.1. Results report both quantities for the populations here.

\subsection{Distributions of fitness effects}\label{app:D_dfe}
Summing Equation~\eqref{eq:VA_MSD} over classes gives the variance a DFE maintains,
\begin{equation}
    V_A^{\text{DFE}} = U_d \sum_k \varphi_k\, s_k\left[1 - \frac{4N_es_k}{e^{4N_es_k}-1}\right] \le U_d\,\bar{s},
\label{eq:VA_forward}
\end{equation}
with equality in the strong-selection limit, where every class contributes $U_d\,s_k$ whatever the shape of the DFE. Hence $s^*$ estimates the arithmetic mean $\bar{s}$. The shape of the DFE matters only through nearly neutral classes ($4N_es_k \lesssim 1$), which contribute less than $U_d\,s_k$. At a fixed mean, more mass in those classes lowers the variance maintained. The analysis uses $U_d\,\bar{s}$ with the DFE assigned to each population~\cite{lin2026} (Methods). This is the upper limit of Equation~\eqref{eq:VA_forward}, so the maintenance test is biased toward passing. DFEs inferred from site-frequency spectra constrain the large-effect tail poorly, so their means are partly extrapolations. The retention factor of each class at balance, $Z_k = 1 - s_k$, and its share of $V_A$, $\nu_k = \varphi_ks_k/\bar{s}$, are derived in Appendix~\ref{app:A_Z}.

\subsection{The reduction at unlinked sites under fast purging}\label{app:D_purge}
Alleles of large effect are purged within a few generations, so their associations with unlinked neutral sites decay faster than through segregation alone. At retention factor $Z_k = 1 - s_k$ the transmission factor of Equation~\eqref{eq:Q} is $Q_k = 2/(1 + s_k)$ in place of 2~\cite{santiago1998}; \cite[Appendix]{charlesworth2012}. Class $k$ carries the share $\nu_k$ of $V_A = U_d\bar{s}$, so the class average of $Q^2V_A$ is
\begin{equation}
    \sum_k \nu_k\,Q_k^2\,V_A = 4U_d\,\bar{s}_\text{eff}, \qquad \bar{s}_\text{eff} = \sum_k \varphi_k\,\frac{s_k}{(1+s_k)^2} < \bar{s}.
\label{eq:D_purge}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:D_purge} replaces $4\kappa V_A$ by $4\kappa U_d\bar{s}_\text{eff}$ in the reduction at unlinked sites, in the multiplicative model or its small-variance approximation. The substitution is exact under random mating and under strict monogamy. With extra-pair paternity, $\kappa$ itself depends on $Z$ (Equation~\eqref{eq:B_kappa_general}), and holding it at its value for $Z = 1$ understates the reduction in $N_e$, by at most 2.3\% for $s_k \le 0.1$. Like truncation of the upper tail of log-fitness (Appendix~\ref{app:A_tail}), purging lowers the reduction in $N_e$ below the multiplicative bound used in the main text. Results give its size at $s^*$.

\subsection{The unlinked limit of background selection}\label{app:D_bgs}
Equation~\eqref{eq:D_purge} is also what background selection becomes at unlinked loci. Consider a selected locus with deleterious mutation rate $u$ per gamete and heterozygous effect $s$. Its mutant frequency is $q \approx u/s$ (mutation--selection balance), so it contributes $2qs^2 = 2us$ to $V_A$, $us$ through each homolog. Its background-selection effect on a neutral site at recombination fraction $r$ is $\exp[-us/(s + r(1-s))^2]$~\cite{hudson1995,nordborg1996}, which at $r = \tfrac12$ simplifies to $\exp\{-[2/(1+s)]^2us\}$, the transmission factor $Q_k = 2/(1+s_k)$ of Equation~\eqref{eq:D_purge} for a class of effect $s$. That expression counts only the copy in cis. At an unlinked locus the homologous copy is reshuffled as often as the cis copy and contributes as much, so the locus lowers $N_e/N$ by the factor $\exp\{-[2/(1+s)]^2\,2us\}$. Summed over loci, with $U_d = \sum 2u$, the exponent is $4U_d\bar{s}_\text{eff}$, which is Equation~\eqref{eq:D_purge} under random mating. A sum over the cis copies alone therefore recovers only half of the reduction at unlinked sites. Within a chromosome the same accounting gives the partition of Equation~\eqref{total_red}, in which the cis variance $v_h = v_i/2$ contributes to the local term and the homologous half stays in the global one. Buffalo and Kern~\cite{buffaloKern2024} count only the cis half; here the homologous half is assigned to the global term. Marsh, Daigle and Johri~\cite{marsh2026} derive the expected reduction in diversity under background selection for a distribution of fitness effects, including the contribution of selected sites on other chromosomes.
