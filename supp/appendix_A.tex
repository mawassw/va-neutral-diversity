\section{Effective Size under Heritable Variance in Log-Fitness}\label{app:unlinkedapp}
This appendix derives the unlinked reduction in effective size used in the main text, under the multiplicative model (Equation~\eqref{eq:ne_mult}) and its small-variance approximation (Equation~\eqref{eq:Q}), and the retention $Z$ of \autoref{sec:msdb}. The model is that of Barton and Etheridge (2011). A population has $N$ breeding adults. A parent's expected number of offspring is proportional to $e^{z}$, where $z$ is its breeding value for log-fitness, additive and Gaussian with variance $V_A$, and realized family sizes are Poisson about that expectation. Only additive effects are included, because dominance and epistatic deviations are not transmitted stably across generations. Mating is random: each offspring's other parent is drawn independently and at random with respect to $z$. The neutral site is unlinked to every selected locus. Pair bonds and extra-pair paternity are treated in Appendix~B.

\subsection{Long-term contributions and effective size}\label{app:A_R}
Follow a neutral gene copy carried by an ancestor with breeding value $z$. We follow the gene copy rather than the ancestor so that the same bookkeeping applies to the pair bond (Appendix~\ref{app:matingapp}) and to linked sites (Appendix~\ref{app:chromosapp}); at an unlinked site both give the ancestor's long-term contribution, because the probability that a gene survives is proportional to the long-term contribution of the individual that carries it (Barton and Etheridge 2011). Its carriers in each later generation inherit on average half of the previous carriers' deviation from the mean, so their deviations are $(z - \bar{z})/2$, $(z - \bar{z})/4$, and so on. Family sizes multiply along the lineage, so the log advantages add, to $(1 + \tfrac12 + \tfrac14 + \dots)(z - \bar{z}) = 2(z - \bar{z})$. Barton and Etheridge (2011, Appendix B) solve the recursion exactly. The expected long-term contribution of the ancestor, normalized so that $\mathbb{E}[R] = 1$, is
\begin{equation}
    R(z) = \exp\left[Q(z - \bar{z}) - \tfrac12 Q^2V_A\right], \qquad Q = 2. \label{eq:A_R}
\end{equation}
The rate of inbreeding is proportional to the second moment of long-term contributions (Wray and Thompson 1990), and the probability that an ancestor is involved in a coalescence goes as the square of $R$ (Barton and Etheridge 2011). Hence
\begin{equation}
    \frac{N}{N_e} = \frac{\mathbb{E}[R^2]}{\mathbb{E}[R]^2}, \label{eq:A_ratio}
\end{equation}
which is one when $V_A = 0$, the Poisson baseline. Because $\log R$ is Gaussian, the ratio is $\exp[\mathrm{Var}(\log R)]$. Under random mating $\mathrm{Var}(\log R) = Q^2V_A = 4V_A$, so
\begin{equation}
    \frac{N_e}{N} = e^{-4V_A}. \label{eq:A_mult}
\end{equation}
This is the $e^{4V_A}$ increase in the rate of coalescence that Weissman and Barton (2012) give for heritable variance in log-fitness at unlinked loci. Under strict monogamy, pairs form at random and a pair with breeding values $z$ and $z'$ has offspring in proportion to $e^{z + z'}$ (Weissman and Barton 2012). The partner's deviation then contributes to the copy's lineage in the same way as the carrier's, so $\log R = 2(z - \bar{z}) + 2(z' - \bar{z}) + \text{const}$. Its variance is $8V_A$, and
\begin{equation}
    \frac{N_e}{N} = e^{-8V_A}. \label{eq:A_mono}
\end{equation}
Weissman and Barton (2012) find the same factor, $e^{-8V_A}$, for the reduction in fixation probability under monogamy. Equation~\eqref{eq:A_mono} is the multiplicative-model form of the pair-mated family variance $C^2 = 2V_A$ of Santiago and Caballero (1995).

\subsection{The small-variance approximation}\label{app:A_first}
For small $V_A$, $R \approx 1 + Q(z - \bar{z})$, and Equation~\eqref{eq:A_ratio} gives $N/N_e = 1 + \mathrm{Var}(R) = 1 + Q^2V_A$, the expansion of Equation~\eqref{eq:A_mult}:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{N_e}{N} = \frac{1}{1 + Q^2V_A}. \label{eq:A_first}
\end{equation}
Robertson (1961) and Santiago and Caballero (1995; reviewed in Wang et al. 2016) obtained this form for any map from breeding value to fitness, valid while $V_A \ll 1$. Their transmission factor is $Q = 2/[2 - Z(1 + r_{mf})]$ (Equation~\eqref{eq:Q}). Here $Z$ is the retention (Appendix~\ref{app:A_Z}), and $r_{mf}$ is the correlation between mates' breeding values, which raises $Q$ when positive. At $Z = 1$ and $r_{mf} = 0$, $Q = 2$, and Equation~\eqref{eq:A_first} becomes $1/(1 + 4V_A)$. Under strict monogamy the same expansion of Equation~\eqref{eq:A_mono} gives $1/(1 + 8V_A) = 1/(1 + Q^2C^2)$. With the mating system included (Equation~\eqref{unlinked_mating}), setting $N_e/N = \delta$ and inverting gives the small-variance ceiling
\begin{equation}
    V_{A,\max} = \frac{1 - \delta}{4\kappa(\alpha)\,\delta}, \label{eq:vamax}
\end{equation}
the variance above which heritable fitness differences alone would remove more diversity than is observed. It holds only where $V_{A,\max} \le 1$, that is $\delta \gtrsim 1/(1 + 4\kappa)$; for smaller $\delta$ the least variance that could account for the deficit is $V_{A,\delta}$ (Equation~\eqref{eq:vamax_mult}).

\subsection{The upper tail of log-fitness}\label{app:A_tail}
Under random mating, $\mathbb{E}[R^2] \propto \mathbb{E}[e^{4z}]$. Weighting the normal density of $z$ by $e^{4z}$ shifts its mean to $\bar{z} + 4V_A$, so the ancestors that carry the reduction lie $4\sqrt{V_A}$ standard deviations above the mean. A 100-fold reduction ($V_A = \ln(100)/4 = 1.15$) rests on ancestors 4.3 standard deviations out, about one in $10^5$. A $10^4$-fold reduction ($V_A = 2.30$) rests on ancestors 6.1 standard deviations out, about one in $10^9$. The expectation is realized only in a population large enough to contain such ancestors, and only if nothing caps their fitness. Any ceiling on fitness truncates the weighted density and lowers $\mathbb{E}[R^2]$: limits on territory or clutch size, or the probability of breeding in Bonnet et al.'s (2022) zero-inflation component. That probability cannot exceed one, so its contribution to log-fitness is bounded above by zero (\autoref{sec:onemodel}). Equations~\eqref{eq:A_mult}, \eqref{eq:A_mono} and their extension to extra-pair paternity (Equation~\eqref{eq:B_mult_pair}) are therefore upper bounds on the reduction that a given $V_A$ produces.

\subsection{The retention \texorpdfstring{$Z$}{Z}}\label{app:A_Z}
The retention $Z$ is the additive variance among parents weighted by their offspring, relative to $V_A$: the fraction of a gene copy's association with its selective background that survives one generation of selection (the $G$ of Santiago and Caballero 1995). A retention per generation presupposes a selected background that is constant over time, which mutation--selection--drift balance provides (Barton 1998, 2000). Under multiplicative fitness, weighting parents by $e^{z}$ is an exponential tilt of the distribution of $z$. Write $K(\theta) = \ln \mathbb{E}[e^{\theta z}]$ for the cumulant-generating function of $z$. The tilted distribution has variance $K''(1)$, so
\begin{equation}
    Z = \frac{K''(1)}{K''(0)}. \label{eq:Zcgf}
\end{equation}
For a Gaussian $z$, $K(\theta) = \theta\bar{z} + \theta^2V_A/2$, and $Z = 1$ at every value of $V_A$: selection shifts the mean of the selected parents by $V_A$ and leaves their variance unchanged. This is the case used for the genome-wide reduction.

Suppose instead that $V_A$ is carried by rare deleterious alleles at mutation--selection balance. A heterozygous carrier of an allele in effect class $k$ has relative fitness $1 - s_k$, a deviation $\ln(1 - s_k)$ in $z$, and the allele has frequency $q_k \approx \mu/s_k$. Cumulants add over unlinked loci, and for a rare allele $K_k''(\theta) \approx 2q_k \ln^2(1 - s_k)\,(1 - s_k)^{\theta}$. Equation~\eqref{eq:Zcgf} then gives, to leading order in $s_k$,
\begin{equation}
    Z = \sum_k \nu_k Z_k, \qquad Z_k = 1 - s_k, \qquad \nu_k = \frac{\varphi_k s_k}{\bar{s}}, \label{eq:Zclass}
\end{equation}
with $\varphi_k$ the fraction of new mutations in class $k$. Each class is retained at its carriers' relative fitness, because the association is lost with the carriers that selection removes (Equation~\eqref{eq:Z}). The weights $\nu_k$ are the classes' shares of $V_A$. The weighted mean is the aggregate $1 - \mathbb{E}[s^2]/\mathbb{E}[s]$ of Santiago and Caballero (1998), but $Q$ and the linked interference are nonlinear in $Z$, so the main text carries the classes separately. Faster purging lowers $Z$, and with it $Q$ and the reduction, below the Gaussian case. Rare beneficial alleles ($s_k < 0$) give $Z_k > 1$.

Equation~\eqref{eq:Zcgf} is specific to multiplicative fitness. If a parent's expected family size were instead linear in its breeding value, $1 + b\,a$ with $\mathbb{E}[a] = 0$, the offspring-weighted variance would be $\mathbb{E}[(1 + ba)a^2] - \mathbb{E}[(1 + ba)a]^2 = V_A + b\mu_3 - b^2V_A^2$, with $\mu_3$ the third central moment of $a$, so
\begin{equation}
    Z = 1 - b^2V_A + b\,\mu_3/V_A. \label{eq:Zarch}
\end{equation}
For additive, Gaussian fitness ($b = 1$, $\mu_3 = 0$), $Z = 1 - V_A$, $Q = 2/(1 + V_A)$ and $N/N_e = 1 + 4V_A/(1 + V_A)^2 \le 2$ at every $V_A$. The floor on $N_e/N$ is an artifact of the map: with $w = 1 + a$, a share $\Phi(-1/\sqrt{V_A})$ of individuals has negative expected fitness, about 8\% at $V_A = 0.5$ and 16\% at $V_A = 1$, which is the range in which the map departs from Equation~\eqref{eq:A_first}. It is not used.
